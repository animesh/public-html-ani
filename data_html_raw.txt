<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Here Lord Krishna describes how the  characteristic  actions  of  the  three
<i>gunas</i> or modes of material nature produce different  results.  According  to
Kapila deva the liberated <i>avatara</i> or  divine  incarnation,  the  results  of
being situated in <i>sattva guna</i> or the mode of goodness is purity, virtue  and
happiness. The results of being  situated  in  <i>raja  guna</i>  or  the  mode  of
passion is desires, activity and attachment. The results of  being  situated
in <i>tama guna</i> or the mode of  ignorance  is  stupefication,pain  and  misery.
Further characteristics of the three <i>gunas</i> from another perspective will  be
elaborated on in chapter 18 beginning at verse 23.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
The result of <i>raja guna</i> or the mode of passion does  not categorically  mean
only pain but denotes pleasure mixed with the pain. Actions are known to  be
in <i>rajas.</i> The Shabda Nirnaya states: In the word  <i>duhkha,  the  dhu</i>  denotes
pain and the <i>kha</i> denotes pleasure.. The Sharakarakshya text   states:  <i>Rajas</i>
verily manifests in small  measures  of  pain  and  pleasure  .  Along  with
pleasure must also come pain; therfore such persons are known as both  happy
and unhappy. Unhappy due to the pain from the effort and happy  due  to  the
pleasure of enjoying the result. Otherwise without such  joy  the  excessive
pain caused by <i>rajas</i> would be the same as the pain  caused  by  <i>tamas</i>  which
has an extremely limited scope for fulfilling  desires.  The  Skanda  Purana
states that <i>tamas</i> or darkness is caused by ignorance and always  results  in
continuous pain.
<p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Thus result of those situated in <i>sattva guna</i> or  mode  of  goodness  at  the
moment of death is birth in a family of enlightened  transcendentalists  who
are <i>atma tattva</i> or knowledgeable of  the  immortal  soul.  Such  a  virtuous
being performs all actions without any  motive  for  rewards  but  only  for
devotion to the Supreme Lord Krishna. The result of  such  actions  is  only
increasing one's <i>bhakti</i> or exclusive loving devotion to  the  Supreme  Lord.
Purity means the absence of any pain so say sages who are in a  position  to
know such matters. The result of those situated in <i>raja guna</i> or the mode  of
passion at the moment of death is birth among those prodiguous families  who
only perform  activities  desirous  of  rewards.  They  actually  engage  in
actions with  the  sole  intent  of  enjoying  the  results  themselves  and
gratifying their dependents and then again and again embarking on  more  and
more ventures developing more and more passion as they  do.  Hence  do  they
perpetuate a mountain of <i>karma</i> or reactions to  actions,  some  good  giving
pleaure  and  some  bad  giving  pain.  Thus  they   perpetuate   themselves
permanently into <i>samsara</i> the perpetual cycle of birth and  death.  Similarly
the result of those situated in in <i>tama guna</i> or the  mode  of  ignorance  at
the moment of death is inertia, nescience, darkness of knowledge.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Now Lord Krishna speaks of  the  different  results  one  receives  and  the
consequences of being in one or  the  other  of  three  <i>gunas</i>  or  modes  of
material nature. The <i>avatar</i> or divine incarnation Kapila deva has  confirmed
that the result characterised by <i>sattva guna</i> or  the  mode  of  goodness  is
purity, happiness and wisdom. The  result  of  <i>raja  guna</i>  or  the  mode  of
passion is characterised by pleasure and pain  due  to  the  combination  of
merit and sin in the activities committed. The result of <i>tama  guna</i>  or  the
mode of ignorance is inertia, nescience and delusion.
<p>
