<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Foods that bequeath longevity,  full  of  energy,  bestow  good  health  and
vigour with happiness and satisfaction are relished by those in <i>sattva  guna</i>
the mode of goodness. Such foods in <i>sattva guna</i> particularly  increase  life
and are juicy, savoury, rich and nourishing, like invigorating  serum  which
remains long in the body and are  agreeable  and  pleasing  to  the  palate.
Foods of this type which are chewed, licked, sucked and drunk  are  relished
by those situated in <i>sattva guna.</i>
<p>
Foods that are excessively bitter,  sour,  pungent,  salty,  spicy,  dry  or
burning are very much liked by those situated  in  <i>raja  guna</i>  the  mode  of
passion. Such foods cause  pain  even  while  eating  them  which  leads  to
distress  of  the  body,  misery,  depression  and  subsequent  disease  and
sickness all produced by these foods.
<p>
Food cooked more than three hours before, that is cold,  tasteless,  without
aroma, stale, decomposed and  foods  that  are  <i>amedhyam</i>  or  forbidden  for
offering to the Supreme Lord such as meat, fish, fowl, eggs, wine,  alcohol,
garlic, onions and mushrooms which come  from  fungus  and  are  impure  are
preferred by those in <i>tama guna</i> the mode of ignorance.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Eating foods that are of <i>sattva guna</i> the mode of goodness  are  pleasurable.
Joyfulness is the immediate result. Whatever continues to be pleasurable  is
joyous to <i>hrdya</i> the heart. Even if all foods are cooked they all do not  get
digested the same. Ghee and honey  are  absorbed  into  the  body  directly,
fruits take an hour to digest and vegetables and grains  up  to  six  hours.
Some foods although bitter are agreeable to health such as <i>kerala</i> or  bitter
melon and some foods although sour like yoghurt are agreeable to  health  if
not taken at night. Both of these are of <i>sattva</i> or  goodness<i>.</i>  Such  is  the
nature of the pious and the saintly and that nature  is  maintained  by  the
intake of foods that are <i>sattva.</i> The Shabda Niranya states: <i>Hridyam</i> is  that
which is pleasing to the heart and makes one desire for  more.  Pleasure  is
what is  pleasing  for  the  moment.  <i>Sukham</i>  or  happiness  is  that  which
continues to keep one joyous long after the activity has  ended.  That  food
which retains its agreeability even after  consuming  repeatedly  is  <i>rasyam</i>
which is delicious and nutritious.
<p>
But when foods are excessively bitter, spicy,  salty.  sour,  pungent,  etc.
and result in discomfort and misery  culminating  in  sickness  and  disease
they are of <i>raja guna</i> the mode of passion. The  food  which  is  eaten  more
than three hours after it is cooked is known as <i>yatama.</i>  When  the  food  is
tasteless it is also called <i>yatayama.</i> Food  that  once  was  flavourful  but
later becomes later has no taste is known as  <i>gatarasyam.</i>  The  Suddhasastra
states that one devoted to serving the Supreme Lord Krishna or  any  of  His
authorised incarnations should thoroughly understand the nature and  quality
of food.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
To those who are situated in <i>sattva guna</i> the mode of  goodness,  foods  that
are of the nature of <i>sattva</i> or goodness such as  milk,  fruits,  grains  and
vegetables are very dear. Such foods promote  long  life,  invigorating  the
body and elevating  the  mind  in  its  function  of  intelligence.  As  was
confirmed earlier in chapter XIV verse XVII:  From  the  quality  of  <i>sattva</i>
<i>guna</i> knowledge arises<i>.</i>  <i>Sattva  guna</i>  facilitates  spiritual  knowledge  and
likewise food that is <i>sattva</i>  also  facilitates  spiritual  intelligence  as
well as good health, strength, happiness and relish. The word <i>sukla</i>  meaning
happiness refers to the pleasure experienced while ingesting  the  food  and
afterwards when it is digesting and assimilating within the body.  The  word
<i>priti</i> means satisfaction that is derived  from  the  intake  of  pure  foods
inspiring congenial interest  in  spiritual  activities.  The  word  <i>rasayam</i>
means succulent, delectable. <i>Snigdhah</i> means  oleaginous,  unctuous.  <i>Sthirah</i>
means nourishing, sustaining. <i>Hrydha</i> means agreeable,  pleasing.  These  are
the qualities of <i>sattva</i> foods which are very much appreciated  by  those  in
<i>sattva guna.</i>  
<p>
Foods that are acidic, bitter, salty, sour, spicy,  pungent,  dry,  hard  or
burning are the foods that those in <i>raja guna</i> are very  much  attracted  to.
The word <i>tiksna</i> refers to food of no value because they are either  to  cold
or to hot. <i>Raksa</i> are foods which are to dry and hard. <i>Vidhahinah</i>  are  those
foods that cause a burning sensation in the body. These foods produce  pain,
distress, sickness and misery and decreases longevity of life  and  increase
desires for more and more <i>rajas</i> or passion.
<p>
The foods liked by those in <i>tama guna</i> the mode of ignorance  are  <i>yata-yaman</i>
meaning stale, foods that are old from the previous day, foods  cooked  more
than a <i>yama</i> which is literally three hours before.  <i>Gata-rasam</i>  means  those
foods which have lost their original  flavour  and  have  become  tasteless.
<i>Puti</i> means putrid, possessing a foul  smell.  <i>Paryusitam</i>  means  decomposed,
unrecognisable. <i>Ucchistam</i> means eating the  remains  of  others  food  after
they  have  eaten.  This  does  not  apply  to  honouring  the  remnants  of
<i>mahaprasadam</i> or sanctified food first  offered  to  Lord  Krishna  and  then
eaten by the spiritual preceptors  who  mercifully  leaves  some  for  their
devotees which is then glorified by  them  as  <i>maha  mahaprasadam</i>.  <i>Amedhyam</i>
means unsanctified foods  due  to  not  having  been  consecrated  by  first
offering it to the Supreme Lord and thus impure. Partaking of foods in  <i>tama</i>
<i>guna</i> such as meat,  fish,  fowl,  eggs,  wine,  alcohol,  etc.  breeds  dark
nescience and great ignorance.
<p>
So in conclusion those fortunate <i>jivas</i> or  embodied  beings  with  spiritual
insight should absolve themselves from the intake  of  foods  in  <i>raja  guna</i>
and <i>tama guna</i> and resort exclusively to foods in <i>sattva guna.</i>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna commences with the  three-fold  divisions  of  foods  with  the
words <i>ayuh-sattva</i> meaning promoting longevity. Food that promotes  spiritual
knowledge which  is  the  quality  of  intelligence  has  been  corroborated
previously in chapter XIV.XVII stating that: Wisdom  verily  arises  out  of
<i>sattva guna</i> the mode  of  goodness.  Whereas  greed  and  lust  are  the  by
products of <i>raja guna</i> the mode of passion  and  lethargy  and  nescience  is
produced by those in <i>tama guna</i> the mode of ignorance. The word <i>bala</i>  meaning
strength and refers to food that gives the  power  to  perform  one's  duty.
Foods that are pleasant to eat, that bestow mental  tranquillity,  that  are
sustaining and easily digestible, that  are  nourishing  and  their  essence
remains invigorating the body even after digested are the  foods  which  are
dear to those endowed with the qualities of <i>sattva guna.</i> 
<p>
Foods which are extremely acidic, salty, spicy, pungent and bitter  such  as
<i>azadirachta indica</i> or burning such as chillies which burn the mouth,  throat
and stomach simply bring about distress and misery which leads  to  sickness
and disease. The prefix <i>aty</i>  meaning  extremely  at  the  end  of  the  word
<i>lavanaty</i> meaning extremely bitter applies to  all  the  descriptions  up  to
<i>vidadhinah</i> which means burning. Such foods are dear to  those  characterised
with <i>raja guna</i>.
<p>
Foods  which  are  half  cooked,  insipid,  stale,   tasteless,   polluting,
contaminated or leftovers from someone  and  foods  that  are  forbidden  to
offer to the Supreme Lord such as meat, fish, fowl,  eggs,  onions,  garlic,
mushrooms, wine, alcohol, etc. are the foods of choice  for  those  situated
in <i>tama guna.</i>
<p>
The obvious conclusion is that  those  who  are  spiritually  motivated  and
desire their best self interests  should  conscientiously  avoid  all  foods
characterised by <i>raja guna</i> and <i>tama guna</i> and  resort  exclusively  to  foods
that are of <i>sattva guna.</i>
<p>
