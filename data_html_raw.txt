<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The branches of this  ancient  banyan  tree  are  the  perishable  <i>jivas</i>  or
embodied beings from Brahma the secondary creator who  lives  for  trillions
of years down to humans who may live for a hundred years down to  an  insect
that may live only for some hours. All regardless  of  their  span  of  life
have their limiting  adjuncts  and  restricted  effects  and  represent  the
branches of this tree. Of those <i>jivas</i> who have the inclination for evil  and
demoniac activities their births will be in the reptile and insect  species.
Those <i>jivas</i> who are oblivious to their divine nature  and  act  like  beasts
will correspondingly take birth in the animal kingdom  and  those  <i>jivas</i>  of
virtuous and pious  nature  nature  will  take  birth  among  the  Brahmins,
Vaisnavas and demigods. All <i>jivas</i> constitute the unlimited branches of  this
ancient  banyan  tree  represented  in  the  mundane   material   existence.
Furthermore it should be understood that  they  are  nutured  by  the  three
<i>gunas</i> or modes  of  goodness,  passion  and  nescience  according  to  their
qualifications and propensities. The tips of the  branches  are  the  senses
and the innumerable sprouts and shoots are the sense objects. The roots  are
spread out above and below with the central primary  tap  root  representing
the Supreme Lord  alone  with  the  roots  below  representing  desires  for
enjoyment and the roots  above  representing   subtle  impressions  of  past
enjoyments.  The  effects  of  such  are  specifird  by  the   words   <i>karma</i>
<i>anubandhani</i> which refers to actions according to the proclivity  to  perform
righteous  or  unrighteous  activities  which   results   in   corresponding
reactions some positive, some benign and some negative. When past  reactions
eventually have been finally exhausted the <i>jiva</i> once  more  takes  birth  in
the world of  humans  directly  related  to  the  influence  of  the  subtle
impressions accumalated from enjoyments experienced in  the  previous  lives
and worlds which are the subtle motivating impetus for the inclinations  and
propensity to experience these activities again. The ability  to  experience
these actions is limited to the worlds of humans alone and so  Lord  Krishna
states <i>manusya-loke</i> meaning the world of men.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Since the unmanifest <i>atma</i> or immortal soul exists in an  impreceptible  sub-
atomic form within the bodies of all <i>jivas</i>  or  embodied  beings,  they  are
described as spread upwards and downwards. The attributes are the  modes  of
material nature, the three <i>gunas</i> of <i>sattva</i> or  goodness,  <i>rajas</i>  or  passion
and <i>tamas</i> or ignorance. The pleasurable  experiences  are  the  sprouts  and
desires are the buds. The main  central  root  is  the  divine  resplendence
representing the Supreme Lord  and  the  subsidary  roots  are  the  results
acquired  due  to  following  righteousness  or  unrighteousness.  This   is
described in the Ballava section.
<p>
<p>
In this ancient <i>asvattha</i> or banyan tree representing material existence  the
<i>brahman</i> or spiritual substratum pervading all existence as manifestation  of
the Supreme Lord is the primary and central  root.  The  subsidary  root  is
<i>prakriti</i> the material substratum pervading physical existence and the  three
<i>gunas</i> are its attributes. The elements earth, water,  fire,  air  and  ether
are the branches and the leaves  are  the  Vedic  hymns.  The  demigods  who
administer universal management are the smaller branches  and  human  beings
are  the  twigs.  From  adherance  or  rejection  of  the  injunctions   and
prohibitions defined in the Vedic scriptures  do  sweet  and  bitter  fruits
results which are the <i>karmas</i> or reactions to the previous actions   one  has
performed.  Righteous  actions  bestow  pleasant  rewards  and   unrighteous
actions  bestow  unpleasant  rewards.   This  ancient  banyan  tree  rewards
liberation and bondage as well depending upon  the  merit  of  righteousness
enacted. It also has fruits, branches and roots that are unmanifest as  well
as those that are apparent and manifest.  This does not  otherwise  come  to
happen neither does it not come not to happen. This means that the cause  is
enveloped in the effect and that the effect  is  containing  the  cause.  In
this way as a tiny seed contains an entire tree and a tree contains  a  tiny
seed the roots and the branches are intertwined eternally.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The branches on this <i>asvattha</i>  tree symbolise  all  the  <i>jivas</i>  or  embodied
beings who arise  due  to  <i>karma</i>  or  reactions  to  actions.  The  downward
branches symbolise humans, animals, plants, etc.  and  the  upward  branches
symbolise the 300 million demigods administering universal management.   All
the branches are part of <i>prakriti</i> thematerial substratum pervading  physical
existence and all are nourished  by  the  three  <i>gunas</i>  or  three  modes  of
material nature which are <i>sattva</i> or goodness, <i>rajas</i> or passion and <i>tamas</i>  or
ignorance. The sprouts on the branches symbolises  desires  for  objects  of
the senses. This <i>asvattha</i> tree has its  roots  in  Brahma-loka  the  highest
material planet and its branches end in the worlds of humans but  there  the
humans are constantly creating new rootlets which are  the  <i>karmas</i>  acquired
from performing actions which are  enjoined  and  prohibited  in  the  Vedic
scriptures.The activities performed as <i>jivas</i>  while  in  bodies  of   humans
exclusively results in higher level or lower level births. By  ignoring  the
injunctions and prohibitions of the Vedic scriptures the results  are  lower
level births such as animals, reptiles, insects, plants.  The  adherance  to
the injunctions and prohibitions of the Vedic scriptures results  in  higher
level births such as the demigods and also human births which still  affords
a <i>jiva</i> the golden opportunity to  spiritually  evolve  and  grow;  sometimes
even achieving <i>moksa</i> or liberation from material  existence  in  their  very
next lifetime.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Describing  the  roots  and  branches  of  the  <i>asvattha</i>  or   banyan   tree
allegorically  to  symbolise  material  existence.  Lord   Krishna   further
explains that the branches that rise  upwards  symbolise  the  higher  level
<i>jivas</i> or embodied beings such as the  demigods  and  humans.  The   branches
that turn downwards are lower level <i>jivas</i> such as animals, birds,  fish  and
plants. The roots extend upwards to Satya loka the highest  material  planet
of Brahma and extends  downwards  also  into  the  worlds  of  humans  where
innumerable new sub-roots manifest which  are  the  <i>karma</i>  or  reactions  to
actions performed by every  human.  The  twigs  are  impressions  from  past
desires and the sprouts are the desired sense objects.  All  parts  of  this
tree  are  within  <i>prakriti</i>  the  material  substratum  pervading   physical
existence and are nourished by the three <i>gunas</i> or modes of  material  nature
which are <i>sattva</i> or goodness, <i>rajas</i>  or  passion  and  <i>tamas</i>  or  nescience.
Those lower level <i>jivas</i> who have degenerated into various forms of  demoniac
entities due to performing evil activities to others  as  well  as  degraded
activities unto themselves inevitably sink into  the  fiery  hellish  worlds
for aeons and aeons of atonement. Contrarily  the  higher  level  <i>jivas</i>  who
adhered to the injunctions and prohibitions of the Veidc scriptures  receive
meritorious births in the heavenly worlds of the  demigods.  Possessing  the
nature of good or evil each <i>jivas karma</i> binds them in <i>samsara</i> the  perpetual
cycle of birth and death and is manifested in <i>manusya</i> the worlds of  humans.
<p>
<p>
The purport is that after exhaustion of all <i>karma</i> and  its  residue  of  the
<i>jiva</i> enjoying ecstatically in the heavenly worlds or suffering in misery  in
the hellish worlds due to either following or ignoring the  injunctions  and
prohibitions of the  Vedic  scriptures.  One  is  rewarded  after  death  by
performing meritorious  deeds  or  punished  by  performing  degraded  deeds
during each human lifetime in pursuit of pleasure and  sense  gratification.
After hundreds of thousands of such lifetimes  these  desires  for  pleasure
become  deep  rooted  tendencies  that  causes   subtle  impressions  to  be
imprinted upon the subtle body of the <i>jiva</i> who takes birth in the worlds  of
humans. These impressions are  so  resolute  that  subconsciously  the  <i>jiva</i>
craves and seeks the  same  pleasures  enjoyed  in  the  previous  life  and
performs the  same  and  similar  activities  in  which  they  had  achieved
fulfillment before.
<p>
