<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
The actions performed by a person as offerings and worship  to  the  Supreme
Lord Krishna are considered inaction as they lead to spiritual  intelligence
and are not bonded in any way to reactions. For one who  has  achieved  <i>atma</i>
<i>tattva</i> or soul realisation all actions are neutralised by knowing  that  one
is not the doer  and  hence  for  them  even  various  natural  actions  are
considered inaction.  The  transformation  of  action  to  inaction  due  to
absence of egoism was previously explained in verse 18. Now Lord Krishna  is
stating that the transformation of action to inaction is always  present  in
the person who performs all their actions in relation to the <i>Brahman</i> or  the
spiritual substratum pervading all existence. One whose mind is absorbed  in
performing all actions as offerings to the <i>Brahman</i> exclusively, attains  the
<i>Brahman</i> without a doubt.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
The essential meaning Lord Krishna is conveying here is the establishing  of
a higher consciousness that sees all activities as offerings to the  <i>Brahman</i>
or the spiritual substratum pervading all existence.  Everything  is  spoken
as  the  <i>Brahman</i>  because  everything  is  emanated  from  the  <i>Brahman</i>  and
everything is contained within the <i>Brahman.</i> It should not  be  assumed  that
the unlimited myriad of  individual  beings  are  in  the  likeness  of  the
<i>Brahman</i> because that would be erroneous. The <i>Brahman</i> is the  whole  and  all
others are infinitesimal parts of the whole  and subservient to  it  because
all comes into existence from the <i>Brahman</i>. In the  Padma  Purana  the  sages
speak that: The Supreme Lord is verily is the essence and the total  of  the
Vedas. Everything is verily the vision of the Supreme  Lord's  consciousness
and the <i>Brahman</i>  is  the  manifestation  of  this  consciousness.  With  the
equanimity of one's intellect <i>Brahman</i> is realised to  be  the  performer  of
all actions.
<p>
Now begins the summation.
<p>
How can pride be relinquished? By offering pride to the <i>Brahman</i>  along  with
other unwanted hindrances. The  paraphernalia  offered  to  the  <i>Brahman</i>  is
called <i>Brahmarpanamas</i>  and  they  include  the  fire  itself,  the  <i>ghee</i>  or
clarified butter and foodgrains offered to the sacred fire as  well  as  the
performer of the <i>yagna</i> or  worship  who  propitiates  the  Supreme  Lord  by
offering oblations. So the <i>Brahman</i> is also the  performing  presribed  Vedic
actions  with  equanimity  in  higher  consciousness.  But  even  with  this
equanimity  all  are  eternally  subservient  to  the  Supreme   Lord.   The
Mahabharata confirms that  only  the  respendent  Supreme  Lord  Krishna  is
independent everything else is dependent upon Him.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Here the word <i>brahmarpanam</i> means the paraphernalia used to  offer  oblations
in <i>yagna</i> or offering of worship to propitiate  the  Supreme  Lord.  All  the
accessories used  in  <i>yagna</i>  are  also  considered  to  be  <i>Brahman</i>  or  the
spiritual substratum pervading all existence, along with  the  offerings  of
ghee and grain seeds, the fire they are  offered  through  as  well  as  the
performer of the offering.  Lord Krishna is explaining that everything  used
in <i>yagna</i> can be considered as part of the <i>Brahman</i>.  One  who  realises  that
the <i>Brahman</i> or the spiritual substratum pervading all existence is  actually
abiding in all actions reaches the <i>Brahman</i> which is  becoming  cognisant  of
the spiritual substratum of reality underlying and pervading all  existence.
This is the consciousness of the actions  from  an  aspirant  for  <i>moksa</i>  or
liberation where the <i>atma</i> or soul is understood  to  be  non-different  from
the <i>Brahman.</i> Consequently actions performed in this  consciousness  are  all
known to be spiritual and thus realised is in itself a means  for  achieving
<i>atma tattva</i>  or  soul  realisation  thus  precluding  any  further  need  to
practice <i>jnana yoga</i> or cultivation of Vedic knowledge.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna explains that all the actions of  such  a  <i>yogi</i>  or  one  whose
individual consciousness is in communion  with  the  ultimate  consciousness
are dissolved but how is this  possible?  It  is  because  such  a  <i>yogi</i>  is
established in the knowledge of the <i>atma</i> or soul  with  full  cognisance  of
every action  being  a  <i>yagna</i>  or  offering  to  the  <i>Brahman</i>  or  spiritual
substratum pervading all existence. As everything in existence is  factually
within the <i>Brahman</i> it can be understood that everything is actually  a  form
of the <i>Brahman.</i> The <i>Brahman</i> or spiritual substratum  of  reality  is  surely
the destination to be achieved by those who are experiencing  it, as  verily
it is eternal and permanent. This is in contrast to one  being  absorbed  in
obtaining the enjoyments of  heaven  which  are  temporary  and  transitory.
Concentration in the <i>Brahman</i> is considered as <i>yagna</i> or propitiation  to  the
ultimate reality.
<p>
