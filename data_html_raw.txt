<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
To the person who again and again brings their mind  under  control  by  the
process of withdrawal from all external designations the bliss  of  <i>yoga</i>  or
the science of the individual consciousness  attaining  communion  with  the
ultimate consciousness manifests  as  soon  as  all  remnants  of  <i>rajas</i>  of
passion have been eliminated. So Lord Krishna is  declaring  that  for  this
superior <i>yoga</i> for whom <i>rajas</i> is no longer an influence  and  whose  mind  is
completely tranquil and content. To this  peerless  <i>yogi</i>  who  has  achieved
<i>atma tattva</i> or realisation of the soul, it  is  without  a  doubt  that  the
supreme bliss comes of its own accord  and  the  ultimate  consciousness  is
attained.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna states <i>brahma-bhutam</i> which means endowed with  the  realisation
of the <i>Brahman</i> or spiritual substratum pervading all existence.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
A peaceful mind means a mind immovably situated  in  constant  mediation  on
the <i>atma</i> or soul. Once this is accomplished it is  quite  natural  that  the
<i>yogi</i>  or  one  perfecting  the  science  of  the  individual   consciousness
attaining communion with the ultimate consciousness by <i>dhyana</i> or  meditation
soon has all blemishes and sins completely purged. The  words  <i>santa-rajasam</i>
means the quality of  <i>rajas</i> or passion has become <i>santa</i> or  peaceful.  Hence
one  becomes  imbued  with  the  qualities  of  the  <i>Brahmam</i>  or   spiritual
substratum pervading all existence and which also refers to the <i>atma</i> and re-
establishs one into their true essence of spiritual  splendour.  To  such  a
<i>yogi</i> comes <i>atma  tattva</i>  or  realisation  of  the  soul  and  exquisite  and
phenomenal transcendental  felicity.  The  word  used  by  Lord  Krishna  <i>hi</i>
meaning verily, is an indeclinable particle which gives  a  reason  denoting
that by the reason of  <i>atma  tattva</i>  being  essentially  blissful  the  <i>yogi</i>
becomes blissful as well.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
After  the  <i>yogi</i>  or  one  perfecting  the   science   of   the   individual
consciousness with the ultimate consciousness has withdrawn  the  mind  from
sense objects and secured it in the <i>atma;</i> then  the  mind  becoming  utterly
serene achieves the supreme bliss that is sublime as  a  matter  of  course.
Lord Krishna speaks of two alternatives: one is <i>santa-rajasam</i> or  free  from
passion and the second is <i>akalmasam</i> or free from the blemishes of desire  in
other words sinless. Such a person is  <i>brahma-bhutam</i>  or  endowed  with  the
realisation of  the  <i>Brahman</i>  or  the  spiritual  substratum  pervading  all
existence.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 6, verse 27 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
