<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
One  should  perform  the  duties  prescribed  in   the   Vedic   scriptures
appropriate  for  ones  stage  in  life.  The  daily  duties  like  praying,
meditating and worshipping are being referred to by Lord Krishna. Action  is
superior to inaction for by abstention from action none of these  activities
can be accomplished. What to say of those, if  one  fails  to  take  actions
even the maintenance of their physical body will not be possible.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
One  should  perform  the  actions  that  are  appropriate  to  one's  <i>varna</i>
or status and <i>ashram</i> or stage in life.  In  Vedic  culture  there  are  four
<i>varnas:  brahmin</i> the priestly  class<i>,  kshatriya</i>  the  royalty  and  warrior
class<i>, vaishya</i> the farmers and businesss classes and <i>sudra</i> the  workers  and
servants of the previous three classes<i>.</i> In  Vedic  culture  there  are  also
four <i>ashrams: bramacharya</i> which is celibate student life, <i>grihasta</i> which  is
married family life, <i>vanaprastha</i> which is semi-retired householder life  and
<i>sannyasa</i> which is complete renunciation of worldy  life  for  meditation  on
God. Due to changing times and the  unseemly  mixture  of  different  varnas
people no longer adhere exclusively to the natural duties  of  their  <i>ashram</i>
as prescribed by the Vedas. This was even happening over 5000 years  ago  at
the end Dvarpara yuga. In the Mahabharata we  see  King  Yudhisthira  noting
that it was becoming exceedingly difficult to determine the <i>varna</i> of  people
due to the mixture of different classes. Therefore it  is  only  by  conduct
and attributes that one can judge what class one belongs to and  not  simply
by what <i>varna</i> one was born into and this conduct is determined  by  actions.
Unless and until the impulse arises for one to surrender themselves in  full
service and devotion to the Supreme Lord Krishna then both <i>varna</i> and  <i>ashram</i>
apply in society. But  after  surrendering  in  devotion  <i>varnashram</i>  is  no
longer applicable or required.
<p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The word <i>nityatam</i> can refer to eternal, regular or even  daily.  An  act  is
eternal if it is connected to the <i>atma</i> which is the eternal  soul.   Actions
merely connected to  <i>prakriti</i>  or  the  material  nature  are  a  persistent
inheritance from one's past activities in the unknown past. Thus to  perform
actions comes easily as a natural phenomena and not subject to the  inherent
dangers  of  possessing  an  impure  mind  or  the  lack  of  sense  control
associated the with <i>jnana yoga,</i>  the  cultivation  of  spiritual  knowledge.
Therefore Lord Krishna is urging Arjuna to perform  <i>karma  yoga</i>  or  actions
without attachment as in his case it is superior to  cultivating  knowledge.
<i>A-karma</i> is non-action which implies cultivating knowledge by the  abstention
of activities. But it is  known  that  it  is  not  possible  to  completely
achieve the actionless state.  Even for the  followers  of  <i>jnana  yoga</i>  the
path of <i>karma yoga</i> is superior because in <i>jnana yoga</i> one must follow a  path
one is naturally not accustomed, is difficult to practice, fraught with  the
risk of dominant senses  and  also  which  does  not  flow  as  an  inherent
tendency like the path of  <i>karma  yoga</i>  where  actions  follow  the  natural
locomotion of the body in the course of daily activities.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Further  it  will  be  shown  that  even  one  who  is  liberated  by  <i>atma-</i>
<i>tattva</i> realisation of the eternal soul; still may be  involved  in  so  many
activities, performing actions without attachment or as a  matter  of  duty.
Thus even <i>atma-tattva</i> can be connected to material  activities  transforming
them to spiritual activities. Hence <i>karma yoga</i> can be superior in this  way.
What can be inferred about the superiority of <i>karma  yoga</i>  over  <i>jnana  yoga</i>
will be evident to one who is actually engaged in practising <i>jnana yoga.</i>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
But an alternative argument could be alright suppose one  is  determined  to
abstain from all actions, then how does one expect to maintain their  bodily
existence which depends on eating and sleeping and  washing  and  exercising
etc. and by which the body is very helpful and useful in  practising   <i>jnana</i>
<i>yoga.</i> The maintenance of one's physical  body  is  absolutely  essential  to
complete the course charted in life until one achieves their goal.  The  way
in which one maintains their body is by actions such as labour  by  rightful
means to acquire funds for performing worship to God by  offerings  of  food
which are subsequently imbued with spiritual potency  and  by  partaking  of
the remnants of such foods solely for bodily sustenance  each  day  maintain
their bodies.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
In the Chandogya Upanisad VII.XXVI.II we find: It is by the purity  of  food
that one's mind becomes purified. When the  mind  is  purified  one  attains
access to the eternal soul dwelling within the heart.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Thus if one desires to desist  from  all  activities  how  will  their  mind
become purified  and how will their bodily needs be maintained? Thus  it  is
obvious that one who practices <i>jnana yoga</i> in order to maintain their  bodily
existence must still continue performing the  daily  and  occasional  duties
prescribed in the Vedas until they reach their goal.  The  same  as  if  one
practised <i>karma yoga.</i> Also in <i>karma yoga</i> the  contemplation  of  <i>atma-tattva</i>
is also included in the conceptions that I am not my body perfoming  actions
and I am not the actual doer of any actions. So for all these reasons  <i>karma</i>
<i>yoga</i> is preferably recommended to <i>jnana yogis.</i>
<p>
<p>
<p>
So the conclusion is for Arjuna to desist and practice <i>karma yoga.</i>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
But if it is still postulated that actions such as the  acquiring  of  funds
involves the my-ness  of this is my money and the I-ness of  I  have  earned
this by the strength of my mind and the  power  of  my  faculties  and  that
these efforts must cause bondage Lord Krishna refutes this argument  in  the
next verse.
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
One should note that by  the  use  of  tvam  Lord  Krishna  is
asserting that Arjuna should be detached from his actions  and
perform his duty. Action is superior to  inaction  because  by
performing  actions  prescribed  in   the   Vedic   scriptures
according to  one’s  occupation  and  stage  in  life  because
accumalated sins are destroyed that one has acquired  in  past
lives.  Realistically  not  performing  actions  is  also  not
practical because without actions one  is  not  able  even  to
maintain the physical body. The particle api  means  even  and
refers to what else more should be clarified.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 3, verse 8 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
