<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The attributes of the  spiritual  worlds  are  briefly  delineated  by  Lord
Krishna. The sun, moon, fire are not required to  illuminate  it  as  it  is
naturally self-lluminating. Once attained a <i>jiva</i>  or  embodied  being  never
returns to the transitory material existence and is no  longer  enslaved  by
the bondage of <i>samsara</i> or the perpetual cycle of birth  and  death.  As  the
Supreme Lords abode is transcendental  and  beyond  the  scope  of  material
existence it is not  subject  to  the  dualities  and  defects  of  material
nature and is eternally perfect in every way.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The light of illumination which manifests from the <i>atma</i> or immortal soul  is
what Lord Krishna is  referring  to  here  as  well.  As  His  transcendntal
spiritual body consists of the <i>atmas</i> of all <i>jivas</i> or  embodied  beings,  He,
His name, His abode and His pastimes are all illuminating. No  sun  or  moon
or fire can illuminate this; for it  is  spiritual  wisdom  that  gives  the
light of consciousness. External luminiousity is only able to  dissolve  the
obscurity that intervenes between the senses perceiving  their  objects.What
reveals the <i>atma</i> is called <i>yoga</i> or  the  individual  conciousness  attaining
communion with the ultimate consciousness. Antagonistic  to  <i>yoga</i>  is  <i>karma</i>
which are subsequent  reactions to previous  actions  and  to  conquer  this
formidable opponent which gains strength from every  action  performed;  one
must fully surrender unto the Supreme Lord and  accept  Him  as  one's  only
refuge. That all illuminating light emanates from Him, it is a part  of  His
splendour  as  a  power  from  Him.  The  sublime  supremacy  of  this   all
illuminating light consists of its potency and efficiency  in  lighting  the
consciousness by spiritual wisdom. Sunlight no matter how  bright,  although
capable of travelling for millions and billions of miles  through  space  is
never able to internally effect illumination in the consciousness as  it  is
only an external phenomena.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Lord Krishna briefly explains  some  characteristics  of  that  imperishable
Supreme state, His eternal abode. There the light of the sun or the moon  or
fire is not necessary because His eternal  abode  is  self-illuminating  and
self-effulgent without any association with material  existence  and  always
beyond the darkness of nescience. This  is  confirmed  in  the  Svetasvatara
Upanisad III.VIII beginning:  <i>veda  hametam  purusam</i>  meaning:  The  Supreme
Lord, more resplendent then the sun, illuminates  all  within  and  without,
only by realising Him is a <i>jiva</i> or embodied being able to transcend  <i>samsara</i>
the perpetual cycle of birth and death, there is no other  possibility.  The
usage of the word <i>mama</i> meaning my dispels any  illusions  of  seperation  or
seperate identity of His abode. It is not an independent entity  nor  is  it
non-different from Him. The Supreme Lord's eternal abode  is  a  portion  of
His divine potency.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 15, verse 6 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
