<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The threefold qualities of the performer of  actions  is  now  described  by
Lord Krishna beginning with <i>sattva  guna</i>  the  mode  of  goodness.  One  who
performs an action devoid of attachment,  free  from  egoism,  equipoise  in
success or failure, with steadiness,  fortitude  and  enthusiasm  is  deemed
situated in <i>sattva guna.</i>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><font size=2>There is no commentary for this verse.</font>
</td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Now Lord Krishna describes the threefold  effects  of  the  three  <i>gunas</i>  or
modes of material nature on a performer of activity  beginning  with  <i>sattva</i>
<i>guna</i> the mode of goodness.
<i>1) muktah-sangah</i> is free from attachment  and  exempt  from  desires  for
results.
<i>2) anaham-vadi</i> is free from egoism as the doer and hence devoid of pride.
<i>3) dhrti</i> is fortitude and  determination  in  surmounting  all  odds  for
accomplishment.
<i>4)  utsaha</i>  is  enthusiasm  and  zeal  in  undertaking  prescribed  Vedic
activities.
The words <i>siddhy-asiddhyor nirvikarah</i> means equipoise in success or  failure
of all activities, realising that all results  are  under  the  auspices  of
<i>karma</i> or reactions to actions and the ultimate will  of  the  Supreme  Lord.
Such consciousness is situated in <i>sattva guna.</i>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Of the three objects worth knowing Lord Krishna speaks of the  performer  of
actions in the three <i>gunas</i> or  modes  of  material  nature  commencing  with
<i>sattva guna</i> the mode of goodness. One  who  is  free  from  the  desire  for
rewards of actions is free from  attachment  to  actions.  Such  a  one  has
dissolved  their  ego  involving  the  performance  of   actions.   Although
possessing  enthusiasm  and  determination  they  are  equipoised  and   not
agitated by the infamy of failure or ecstatic by the adulation  of  success.
Those possessing such attributes are invariably situated in <i>sattva guna.</i>
<p>
