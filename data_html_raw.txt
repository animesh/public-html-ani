<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Anticipating that the question may arise of how it is  practically  possible
to accept only the positive and reject the negative, Lord  Krishna  responds
by stating that one whose mentality is unfettered to  anything  mundane  and
material, devoid of attachment. Whose mind has conquered the false  lure  of
desires and attraction to the objects  of  the  senses.  Who  is  bereft  of
egotism and is renounced and  situated fully in  <i>sattva  guna</i>  the  mode  of
goodness; such a one achieves no reaction to actions,  purity  of  mind  and
hence absence of activities. Although such a  one  may  perform  actions  in
actuality it is non-action because there  is  no  conception  as  the  doer.
Chapter five verse 8 has already stated: That one  who  knows  the  absolute
truth understands that they  factually  are  not  the  doer  and   verse  13
states: The self controlled being who renounces all actions  in  their  mind
remains tranquil and serene and is considered to be topmost.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Perfection in renunciation entails relinquishing  all  desires  for  rewards
thereof which is perfect equanimity. Perfection in renunciation  of  actions
is abandoning all actions not connected to the Supreme Lord Krishna.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord  Krishna  explains  that  when  <i>buddhi</i>  or  spiritual  intelligence  is
unfettered meaning unattached  to  desires  and  rewards  and  the  mind  is
controlled  and  senses  conquered,  with  no   conceptions   of   doership,
established  in  renunciation  even  while   performing   prescribed   Vedic
activities one attains the supreme  serene  state  of  <i>bhakti</i>  or  exclusive
loving devotion unto the Supreme Lord by <i>dhyana</i> or meditation  in  communion
with Him internally as well as propitiation to Him with one's physical  body
externally such as <i>japa</i> or chanting His divine, holy names  in  ecstasy  and
promulgating His glories everywhere one goes  throughout  creation.  By  the
wonderfulness of <i>bhakti</i> the mind  and  senses  are  already  controlled  and
conquered quite easily and have  surpassed  the  limits  of  <i>jnana  yoga</i>  or
facilitating communion with the Supreme Lord by knowledge.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Perfection in the form of  absolute  knowledge  is  performing  propitiation
unto the Supreme Lord Krishna or any  of  His  authorised  incarnations  and
expansions as ordained in Vedic  scriptures  in  accordance  to  ones's  own
natural propensity. Now He presents the resultant rewards  with  the  words:
One whose mind is unfettered and free from  attachment  to  anything,  whose
senses are totally subdued and completely under control, whose  desires  and
cravings have  all evaporated and cease to exist; such a one can  reach  the
Supreme state of perfection. This Supreme state is the highest devotion  and
is flowing and constant like the  purifying  flow  of  the  celestial  river
Ganges, in the form of constant meditation  and  continuous  remembrance  of
the divine form, divine qualities, divine sweetness and divine  pastimes  of
the effulgent Supreme Lord.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 18, verse 49 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
