<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
If the  mind  should  become  unfocused  due  to  the  influence  of  latent
impressions in the mind from past activities; then  one  unperturbed  should
firmly bring the mind back by concentration and refocusing meditate  on  the
<i>atma</i> or soul while withdrawing the mind away from the  external  impressions
of the subtle body. This will manifest gradually by degrees and  should  not
be expected to happen immediately. The way of  confirming  if  the  external
impressions of the subtle body have been evaporated is being given  by  Lord
Krishna with the words <i>na kincid api cintayet</i>  meaning  one  will  think  of
nothing  but  the  <i>atma.</i>  Having  attained  communion  with   the   ultimate
consciousness perceived spontaneously by a focused  and  tranquil  mind  one
should desist even from all  conceptions  of  meditation  that  present  the
person meditating as different form the object of  meditation  or  otherwise
as the individual consciousness being different from the <i>atma.</i>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The word <i>sarvan</i> means all desires in every sphere  of  endeavour.  The  word
<i>asesatah</i> means complete cessation of all desires. The word  <i>manasaiva</i>  means
by the sole strength of the  mind  only  is  restraint  possible.  Spiritual
intelligence  is  the  instrument  for  restraining  the  mind  as  well  as
restraining the sense. This is what Lord Krishna is indicating.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Desires are of a two-fold nature. <i>Sparsa-ja</i> which arise  from  the  impulses
of the physical body or and <i>sankalpa-ja</i> which arise  from  the  impulses  of
the mind or mental origin. <i>Sparsa-ja</i> includes desires for cold or  for  hot,
or for sweet or for  salty,  or  the  lack  of  such.  <i>Sankalpa-ja</i>  includes
desires for wealth, fame, dominion, progeny and such. With great  effort  it
is possible to abandon the desires of the mind by avoiding  to  think  about
them. It is also possible to resist the  sensations  of  pleasure  and  pain
with an attitude of indifference; but between the two  the  desires  of  the
mind are more easy to abandon because  it  is  not  possible  to  avert  the
sensations of  the  body.  Thus  it  is  necessary  to  comprehensively  and
systematically neutralise  the  senses  from  their  external  corresponding
sense  objects.  This  should  be  undertaken  gradually  by  degrees   with
determination and a resolute will. Then in due course of time the mind  will
be weaned from all things except the  eternal  <i>atma</i>  or  soul  and  absorbed
exclusively in the <i>atma,</i> one thinks of nothing else.  This  is  the  meaning
Lord Krishna intended.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna uses the words <i>sanaih sanair</i> meaning gradually by  degrees  one
who has completely withdrawn their senses  from  all  external  distractions
fixes the unflickering mind on the <i>atma</i>  or  soul  with  firm  and  resolute
determination that they will in due course of  time  most  assuredly  become
established  in  <i>yoga</i>  or  the  science  of  the  individual   consciousness
attaining communion with the ultimate consciousness.  By  thinking  in  this
way there will be no slackness in  their  practice  or  diversion  in  their
enthusiasm although on occasion there might arise  delays  and  obstructions
on their way still they should think only on the <i>atma.</i>
<p>
