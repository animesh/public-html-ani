<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Now the science of transmigration of the <i>atma</i> or immortal soul from <i>jiva</i>  to
<i>jiva</i> or embodied being is explained. When the postive and negative <i>karma</i>  or
reactions to  actions  that  one  has  performed  has  been  calculated  and
tabulated along with the types of food one has eaten throughout  their  life
a <i>jiva</i> obtains birth in an appropriate womb and acquires a new  body  taking
along with it in a subtle body the mind and the  senses  from  the  previous
body. This can be understood by the analogy Lord Krishna gives of  a  breeze
transporting to another location the fragrances of the flowers it  has  come
into contact with. So the subtle body of the <i>jiva</i> containing  the  mind  and
the senses transports itself from body to body.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
When a <i>jiva</i> or embodied being due to the mercy  of  the  Vaisnava  spiritual
master, attains the auspiciousness of <i>bhakti</i> or  exclusive  loving  devotion
unto the Supreme Lord Krishna at that  time  the  Supreme  Lord  Himself  as
<i>paramatma</i> the Supreme Soul inconceivably resides simultaneously  within  the
etheric heart of every <i>jiva</i>  or  embodied  being  commences  to  direct  the
actions of that blessed disciple of His  servitor,  the  Vaisnava  spiritual
master, who guides and instructs through his  words  and  actions.  Although
the Supreme Lord is continuously entering and departing each and every  womb
along with every <i>jiva</i> and <i>atma,</i> he does not desire any recompense for  this.
In the Moksa Dharma known also as the Mahabharata it is stated: Even  though
the <i>jiva</i> witnesses the temporal emotions,  merits  and  demerits  and  knows
what is ultimately beneficial it is powerless to oppose  them.  Even  though
moving hither and thither to the four corners of the planet,  enjoying  here
and suffering there, all actions  are  actually  energised  by  the  Supreme
Lord. Even when conquering and winning if one considers themselves  a  great
personality, factually they are only performing as ordained by  the  Supreme
Lord. Even as a vehicle controlled by someone moves  a  passenger  from  one
location to another. In the same manner  the  <i>jiva</i>  transmigrates  from  one
body to another controlled by the potency of the Supreme Lord.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Whatever body a <i>jiva</i> or  embodied  being  is  subjected  to  reside  in  and
whatever body they depart from; the <i>atma</i> or immortal soul  is  automatically
accompanied by the five senses and the  mind.  Lord  Krishna  explains  this
process as similar to  a  breeze  which  picks  up  the  scents  of  flowers
carrying them through through the air from one location to another.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
When and how the mind and senses accompany the  <i>jiva</i> or  embodied  being  in
the process of transmigration from one body at the time of death to  another
at the time of conception is clarified by Lord Krishna. The purport is  that
wherever the <i>jiva</i> departs from a body and whenever it is compeled to  accept
another body the  <i>atma</i> or immortal soul migrating from one body to  another,
arrives with the subtle forms of the mind and  senses  in  tact  to  perform
their functions through the physical body which has  been  allotted  due  to
<i>karma</i> or reactions to previous actions.  The  Supreme  Lord  clarifies  this
with the analogy of the wind carrying  the  scents  of  flowers  to  various
locations.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 15, verse 8 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
