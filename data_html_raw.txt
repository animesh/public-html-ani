<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The binding nature of <i>tamas</i> or mode of  ignorance  and  its  characteristics
are now stated by Lord Krishna. The word <i>tamas</i> means darkness and is  arises
from that  part  of  <i>prakriti</i>  or  the  material  substratum  that  pervades
physical existence, that possesses the power of <i>mohanam</i>   being  that  which
deludes into illusion, concealing the true perception. This  power  has  the
ability to confound and bewilder all <i>jivas</i> or embodied beings.  Hence  <i>tamas</i>
binds imperiously the <i>jivas</i> through <i>pramada</i> or madness, <i>alasya</i> or  indolence
and <i>nidrabhis</i> or sleep. <i>Pramada</i> can also includes  listlessness  and  mental
fragmentation. <i>Alasya</i> can also include laziness and dullness  and  <i>nidrabhis</i>
can also include inaction of the mind due to inebriation or exhaustion.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
That by which  one  becomes  obscure  is  called  born  of  <i>ajnana</i>  or  non-
awareness. This is what is indicated by the words <i>alasya</i>  or  indolence  and
<i>nibhadhnati</i> or iertia.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna describes the  word  <i>ajnana</i>  meaning  ignorance  which  is  the
antithesis of wisdom. The word  <i>jnana</i>  meaning  knowledge  is  the  precise,
accurate perception whereas <i>ajnana</i> is the perverted, inaccurate  perception.
The word <i>tamas</i> means darkness denoting the  darkness  of  ignorance  and  is
diametrically opposite to the light of knowledge. The word <i>mohanam</i>  is  that
which deludes into illusion and the darkness of  ignorance.  This  <i>tamas</i>  is
also the  cause  of  <i>pramada</i>  or  madness  and  listlessness  which  is  the
inability to focus having a fragmented attention span. It is also the  cause
of  <i>alasya</i> or  indolence  slothlike  laziness  which  is  the  inability  to
properly execute any  endeavor  properly  and  <i>nidrabhih</i>  or  sleep  is  the
disinclination of the senses to function  terminating  all  activities.  All
these things <i>nibadhanti</i> or binds  one  to  the  material  existence  without
reprieve.The  sublation  of  the  external  senses  constitutes  the   dream
state;but when the mind is  also  sublated  then  the  dream  state  becomes
sleep.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Now Lord Krishna defines <i>tama guna</i> or the mode of ignorance. The word  <i>tamas</i>
means darkness  and is  typified by nescience and inertia. It is subject  to
the greatest delusion that imagines a permanent and pleasant  experience  in
a  temporary  and  unpleasant  existence.  Those  humans  that   look   upon
themselves as being their physical body like the animals do and  who  accept
their physical body as being all that they are all  hopelessly  situated  in
<i>ajnana</i> or absence of knowledge which is the antithesis of <i>jnana</i>  or  wisdom.
<i>Jnana</i>  is  the  accurate  precise  understanding  whereas  <i>ajnana</i>   is   the
inaccurate perverted understanding. The inaccurate  perverted  understanding
binds the <i>jiva</i> or embodied being through <i>pramada</i> or madness  which  includes
listlessness  and  bewilderment,  <i>alasya</i>   or   indolence   which   includes
sluggishness and laziness and <i>nidrbhis</i> or sleep which includes  inebriation.
Sleep is the cessation of bodily organs arising  from  mental  and  physical
exhaustion. All of these are permanently situated in <i>tama guna</i> and  invoking
ignorance tightly bind the <i>jiva.</i>
<p>
