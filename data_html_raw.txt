<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Commencing in chapter two, verse eleven Lord Krishna instructs  Arjuna  that
he is lamenting for what should not be grieved for. A pattern has  been  set
in motion that gives a sense of discrimination  between  the  soul  and  the
physical body  and  has  been  instructed  as  a  way  to  attain  <i>moksa</i>  or
liberation. So <i>jnana yoga</i> or a requisite  mentality  towards  the  soul  has
been taught, now learn how to apply this in respect to requisite  activities
in <i>karma yoga</i> as was explained beginning in chapter two, verse  thirty-nine.
Although <i>jnana yoga</i> and  <i>karma-yoga</i>  have  been  explained  in  detail,  the
relationship between the two  has  not  been  established  as  to  which  is
superior and which is subordinate.  In  regard  to  discrimination  and  the
cultivation of spiritual knowledge and being informed that they lead one  to
<i>moksa</i> or liberation; Arjuna came to the conclusion that Lord Krishna  valued
this path to be superior to <i>karma yoga</i> or the path of actions. But  if  this
was true then Arjuna was bewildered because he did not understand  why  Lord
Krishna was urging him to engage in a ghastly, horrific  war  by  repeatedly
telling him to arise and fight
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><font size=2>There is no commentary for this verse.</font>
</td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The ultimate purpose of the Bhagavad-Gita is to show in a  clear  and  lucid
manner that one-pointed   loving  devotion  to  the  Supreme  Being  who  is
glorified and proclaimed in the Vedas is the singular and paramount goal  to
be attained by all human beings specifically and for all  living  beings  in
general.  Who is proclaimed  in  the  Upanisads  as  the  singular  goal  to
achieve by the aspirants for <i>moksa</i> or liberation  from  material  existence.
Who  is  declared  in  the  Puranas  as  being  destitute  of  all  defiling
characteristics such as <i>avidya</i> or  nescience.  This  Supreme  Being  who  is
possessing boundless and  unlimited  magnificent  and  glorious  attributes.
<i>Bhakti yoga</i> or loving devotional service is exclusively only in relation  to
the Supreme Lord Krishna or any of His authorised  <i>avatars</i>  or  incarnations
as revealed in Vedic scriptures. In order to achieve full success in  <i>bhakti</i>
<i>yoga</i> it is essential to achieve <i>atma tattva</i> or realisation of one's  eternal
soul. The  <i>atma</i>  or  soul  is  one's  eternal  nature  beyond  all  material
conceptions and is a necessary preliminary step. <i>Atma tattva</i>  is  ultimately
attained by <i>jnana yoga</i> or the cultivation of spiritual  knowledge  from  the
Vedic scriptures which is generated by <i>karma  yoga</i>  or  the  performance  of
prescribed Vedic  activities  without  attachment.  These  prescribed  Vedic
activities are established on the foundation that the <i>atma</i>  is  eternal  and
can never be modified or destroyed as delineated so clearly by Lord  Krishna
in chapter two.
<p>
Brahma has stated  that  the  acquisition  of  <i>paravidya</i>  or  the  knowledge
required to attain the Supreme Being is known as <i>bhakti</i> or  loving  devotion
and that <i>dahara-vidya</i> or that this  method  of  meditation  is  required  to
realise the Supreme Being, who resides within  the  etheric  region  of  the
heart. In the Chandogya Upanisad it is given that  the  realisation  of  the
<i>atma</i> within one's own heart is the first ancillary step by the God  aspirant
to attaining realisation of the Supreme Being Himself. Then the  incorporeal
and non-material reality of the <i>atma</i>  and  its  eternal  nature  beyond  the
consciousness of waking, sleeping and dreaming  is  completely  transcended.
Thus does the phenomenal  nature  of  the  <i>atma</i>  emerging  from  within  the
physical body, radiant  in  ineffable  light  shining  in  its  natural  and
wonderous effulgence. In the  Katha  Upanisad  this  subject  is  also  well
elucidated as given in the following description:  By  perceiving  <i>paramatma</i>
which is the Supreme Soul existing  simultaneously  within  all  beings  and
then by discrimination obtained after realisation of  the  individual  <i>atma.</i>
The enlightened one delivers themselves from attraction and  repulsion,  joy
and grief and all other  dualities.  Communion  with  the  Supreme  Soul  is
communion with the Supreme Being which is the goal to  be  attained  through
the discrimination derived directly from <i>atma  tattva.</i>  This  confirms  that
<i>atma tattva</i> or realisation of  the  soul  is  an  essential  ingerdient  and
constituent component of the Supreme Being which is attained  by  <i>bhakti</i>  or
loving devotion. Another  example  is:  Knowing  the  magnificent  and  all-
pervading nature of <i>paramatma</i> the  Supreme  Soul,  the  enlightened  one  no
longer grieves or laments which reveals the natural potency of the  <i>atma</i>  to
relieve one of all mundane dross  due  to  the  influence  of  the  material
existence<i>.</i>
<p>
The eternal soul known as the <i>atma</i> is not gainable merely by  hearing  about
it. Nor is it obtainable by discoursing about it nor  is  it  attainable  by
meditating exclusively on it. The <i>atma</i> is attainable solely  to  those  whom
the <i>atma</i> alone elects to attain it. Whomsoever the <i>atma</i> selects  as  an  act
of sovereign grace alone the <i>atma</i> reveals itself to such a one. Who is  such
a one? It is none other than that person who has  discriminatory  wisdom  as
their charioteer, who has the reins of their mind controlling the steeds  of
the senses, who has the actual competence to  achieve  <i>moksa</i>  or  liberation
and attain the coveted goal of loving devotion to the Supreme Lord.
<p>
So in conclusion what  we  have  just  presented  in  essence  form  is  the
ultimate purpose  of  the  Bhagavad-Gita.  From  chapter  three  along  with
chapters four, five and six the path for the  aspirant  is  given  beginning
with the very first question in this chapter. The  means  of  cognition  and
the method of realisation along with the meditation required to  achieve  it
will be delineated and forthcoming.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
So in review chapter  one  introduced  the  setting  in  which
Arjuna’s grief and lamentation arose as an apparent cause  for
spiritual instructions from the Supreme Lord Krishna. Then  in
order to remove this grief and lamentation in chapter two Lord
Krishna  reveals  the  eternal  nature  of   the   soul,   the
discrimination between body and the soul, karma yoga, the path
of actions without attachment, jnana yoga the  cultivation  of
spiritual knowledge and the restaint  of  the  senses,  taking
refuge of the Lord, equaninity of mind and the  attainment  of
moksa or liberation have all been delineated. Thus the essence
of Srimad Bhagavad-Gita has been presented in chapter two in a
condensed form.  The  remaining  sixteen  chapters  should  be
understood to be an  extension  of  the  topics  discussed  in
chapter two.
<p>
Here in this verse Arjuna  is  thinking  that  he  was  blamed
earlier in chapter two, verse 11 for lamenting for  those  who
should not be grieved for.  He  was  subsequently  tutored  in
jnana yoga, the path of knowledge to alleviate this error. Now
Lord Krishna is presenting a way to allieviate this error from
the point of view of karma yoga, the path of  actions  without
attachment. In chapter two, verse thirty-nine he  is  told  he
has the right to perform actions but not to desire  the  fruit
of action. In chapter two, verse forty-seven  and  forty-eight
he is instructed not to be attached to  inaction  and  perform
duties with equanimity, respectively.  Then  in  chapter  two,
verse fifty-three he is told how  the  mind  becomes  in  this
state. Then from verse fifty-five until the end of the chapter
two Lord Krishna  gives the proofs to show the superiority  of
the cultivation of spiritual  knowledge  which  leads  to  the
ultimate truth in the last verse of this  chapter  where  once
one having attained even at  a  second  before  death  attains
liberation from the material existence. But in spite of  these
instructions Arjuna was still being instructed  to  arise  and
fight. These seemingly contradictory instructions caused  some
confusion in Arjuna’s mind that he was  anxious  to  have  the
omniscient and omnipotent supreme Lord Krishna dispel.
