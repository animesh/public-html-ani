<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
The question may  be  raised  that  if  persons  practising  <i>karma  yoga</i>  or
prescribed Vedic activities without desiring rewards have anyway to  achieve
<i>atma tattva</i> or realisation of the soul  through  renunciation  it  would  be
better to  renounce  actions  right  from  the  start.   To  alleviate  this
question Lord Krishna advises that it is  very  difficult  and  perilous  to
attempt this without  performing  <i>karma  yoga</i>  without  desire  for  rewards
beforehand because the mind will not  have  been  purified.  Contrarily  the
<i>munir</i> or elevated sage devoted to actions  devoid  of  ego  easily  purifies
their mind and realises the <i>Brahman</i> or spiritual  substratum  pervading  all
existence quickly in the same lifetime. This is  why  it  is  iterated  that
<i>karma yoga</i> is superior to renunciation of  action  because  it  can  not  be
maintained without first performing <i>karma yoga.</i> That is why it  is  said  by
the author of the Varttika that: One even sees the minds  of  <i>sannyasins</i>  or
complete renunciates in abnegation agitated by  material  desirous  and  are
externalised  with  thoughts  polluted  by  contact  with  material  nature,
becoming careless, malicious, and quarrelsome.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna is reconfirming that renunciation is advisable  for  equanimity
of mind for without it the possibility  of  <i>moksa</i>  or  liberation  from  the
cycle of birth and death in the material existence will  not  manifest.  The
desire for sense objects and  the  motivation  for  rewards  only  leads  to
misery. <i>Moksa</i> itself is should be the goal of all endeavours  as  all  other
goals are of little importance as they are material and  transitory  and  in
one's possession for only a limited time.  The  Padma  Purana  states:  That
except for the goal of  <i>moksa</i>  all  other  goals  are  not  even  worthy  of
consideration. When an effort  is  able  to  bestow  superior  results,  the
bestowal of inferior results is of no consequence.  The  primary  effect  of
equanimity is renunciation thus the word <i>munir</i> is given meaning one who  has
renounced. Thus it is said that they alone are known as renunciates who  are
devoid of desire and anger.
<p>
Now begins the summation.
<p>
The means of <i>moksa</i> or liberation is said to be the state of  equanimity  and
its effect is renunciation. That which is always first offered to the  lotus
feet of the Supreme Lord for His pleasure is true renunciation and no  other
forms of renunciation are commendable. In the Agni Purana  is  stated:  That
without renouncing the desire of rewards  for  one's  actions  whatever  one
might offer unto the Supreme Lord has no  merit  and  the  rewards  received
from all forms of renunciation contrary for the satisfaction of the  Supreme
Lord are  similar to the pleasures of hell. Hence it has been declared  what
is called renunciation know that to  be  equanimity.  Since  equanimity  has
been clarified to be of such  paramount  importance  there  is  no  need  to
emphasise it further.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna affirms that the aspirant  who  performs  renunciation  of  the
rewards of actions  and  the  aspirant  who  practices  <i>jnana  yoga</i>  or  the
cultivation of Vedic knowledge, for both of them <i>atma tattva</i> or  realisation
of the soul is difficult to achieve  without  the  help  of  <i>karma  yoga</i>  or
performing prescribed Vedic  activities  without  desire  for  rewards.  The
compound words <i>yoga-yuktah</i> is  one  who  has  achieved  communion  with  the
ultimate  consciousness  by  <i>karma  yoga</i>  or  performing  prescribed   Vedic
activities without attachment. The word <i>munir</i> or sagacious  is  one  who  is
focused on meditating on the <i>atma</i> or soul. Such a <i>muni</i> easily traverses  the
path of <i>karma yoga</i> receiving <i>atma tattva</i> or realisation of the soul as  well
as realisation of the <i>Brahman</i> or  the  spiritual  substratum  pervading  all
existence. But the person who is inclined  to  perform  <i>jnana  yoga</i>  or  the
cultivation of Vedic knowledge finds this path extremely difficult and  hard
to maintain. Because of this it takes an exceptionally long time  to  attain
the <i>atma</i> by this discipline.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
The question may be asked that if the path of <i>karma yoga</i> or prescibed  Vedic
activities  without  desiring  rewards  is  anyway  realised   through   the
renunciation of action then why not start with  renunciation  of  action  in
the beginning. To alleviate this doubt Lord  Krishna  replies  that  without
<i>karma yoga</i> it is impossible to achieve purity of mind and in the absence  of
a pure mind it is  very  difficult  and  even  perilous  as  the  chance  of
disturbance and agitation are prevalent. Whereas the purified  mind  of  the
<i>munir</i> or elevated sage endowed with  equanimity  who  practices  <i>karma  yoga</i>
very soon perceives  the  <i>Brahman</i>  or  spiritual  substratum  pervading  all
existence and achieves <i>atma tattva</i> or realisation of the soul as well.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 5, verse 6 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
