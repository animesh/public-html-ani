<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
The unqualified renunciate  who  in  ignorance  attempts  to  perform  <i>jnana</i>
<i>yoga</i> the cultivation of spiritual knowledge is  being  censored  now.  In  a
strong sense Lord Krishna states: that fool who  by  forcefully  restraining
the senses under the pretext of meditation but  is  inwardly  reflecting  on
the objects of the senses is a cheat and a charlatan. Being impure the  mind
of such an impostor lacks the tranquillity and  lucidity  to  practice  such
meditation.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><font size=2>There is no commentary for this verse.</font>
</td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
Whosoever without first  unburdening  themselves  of  all  their  sins,  who
without having first gained mastery  of  their  senses  and  without  having
first achieved self-control of the  mind.  If  such  a  person  pretends  to
strives  for  <i>atma-tattva</i>  or  self-realisation  of  the  soul,  with  their
thoughts dwelling on sense objects due to attachment  for  them  instead  of
focusing on the eternal soul. Then the conduct of such a one  is  duplicious
and false and they are a charlatan. Any person acting in this  way  is  only
deceiving themselves by their own deception and surely  will  never  succeed
in realising the eternal soul.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<i><u>Bhagavad-Gita</u></i>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
If actions themselves are dependent upon  the  organs  of  the
senses such as  eyes,  nose,  ears  etc.,  then  how  can  one
practice to control them. One who is able  to  restrain  their
body from sense objects still may find their mind attached  to
sense objects. This is due to impurities caused by passion and
desires from  past  lifetimes.  Such  a  person  is  called  a
charlatan. The sense is that the  mind  cannot  be  free  from
attachment  to  sense  objects  because  of  an   absence   of
perfection in karma yoga by performing actions without  desire
thereby destroying the myriads of  sins  collected  from  time
immemorial. Until and unless this has been achieved knowers of
the Vedic scriptures call such a one a hypocrite or  charlatan
and all the efforts of such a charlatan although appearing  as
a renouncer is of no use for moksa or liberation.
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 3, verse 6 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
