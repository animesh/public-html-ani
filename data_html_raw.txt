<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
One who is desireless, free from  attraction  and  repulsion,  who  performs
every action as an act of <i>yagnaya</i> or as offering and worship to the  Supreme
Lord Krishna.  For  one  situated  in  <i>karma  yoga</i>  or  the  performance  of
prescribed Vedic activities  all  their  actions  become  inaction  and  all
reactions along with any impressions are  dissolved.  For  one  situated  in
<i>jnana yoga</i> or the cultivation of Vedic knowledge who  has  already  attained
<i>atma  tattva</i>  or  realisation  of  the  soul;  the  word   <i>yagnaya</i>   denotes
worshipping the Supreme Lord to set an exemplary example in society.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Now Lord Krishna summarises the conclusion of the previous five verses.  The
compound words <i>gata- sangasya</i> means being freed from  desires  for  rewards.
The  word  <i>muktasya</i>  means  being  free  from  bodily  vanity.   The   words
<i>jnanavasthita.cetasah</i>  means  one  who  has  communion  with  the   ultimate
consciousness residing in the heart.
<p>
Now begins the summation.
<p>
Being free from all dependence means to take complete refuge in the  Supreme
Lord and to be totally under His protection.  Liberated  means  to  be  free
from all sense of pride.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
By one's mind being entirely engrossed in <i>atma tattva</i>  or  soul  realisation
the  attachment  to  worldly  things  has  naturally  been   withdrawn.   So
automatically conceptions of  ownership  proprietorship  are  abandoned  and
realising  the  reality  of  the  Supreme  Being  and  the   importance   of
propitiation to Him one lives their life in such a  way  that  every  action
they perform is a  <i>yagna</i>  or  worship  and  offering  to  the  Supreme  Lord
Krishna. All past accumulated  reactions  resulting  from  previous  actions
which bind  one  so  terribly  to  the  material  existence  are  completely
evaporated without any residue. The  spiritual  intelligence  of  performing
all actions in the perspective of the <i>atma</i> or  soul  rather  than  from  the
perspective of being immersed within the sense gardens  of  material  nature
has explained.  The next  verse  reveals  the  even  higher  perspective  of
comprehending that  spirit  behind  all  activities  and  their  factors  of
performance is the Supreme Spirit the Supreme Lord.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
It is being stated by Lord Krishna  why  a  <i>yogi</i>  or  one  whose  individual
consciousness is in communion with the ultimate consciousness is  not  bound
by actions although engaged in actions.  Because  such  a  one  is  free  of
passion and likes and dislikes. Devoid  of  attachment  to  the  rewards  of
actions due to one's mind being firmly fixed in the bliss  of  the  <i>atma</i>  or
soul and  who  performs  all  actions  completely  according  to  the  Vedic
injunctions as a <i>yagna</i> or an offering of worship  for  the  propitiation  to
the Supreme Lord. This is why such a person is never bound. When such a  one
is not interested in achieving merit or distinction how can  they  ever  get
demerits by being tempted to perform any prohibited act. Although  the  doer
is known to hold equal shares of merit or demerit with the one  who  caused,
the inspirer and the on who proposed in positive or negative  results.  Such
a <i>yogi</i> is  never  implicated  because  others  perform  actions  solely  for
desires of rewards while the <i>yogi</i> does not.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 4, verse 23 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
