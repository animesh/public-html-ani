<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The reason why a person is free from delusion and has there mind fixed in
transcendence is now being given by Lord Krishna. The word sparsesu means
sense pleasures and those attached to them are called spharsa. But one
whose mind is unattached and is withdrawn internally ceases to crave, yearn
and desire for indulging in the objects of the senses. Such a person
attains the sublime and serene bliss that is the nature of the atma or
soul. Having attained the wonder of atma tattva or realisation of the soul
one continuously identifies with the Brahman by being immersed in this
consciousness and experiences never ending bliss.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
In this verse Lord Krishna again  clarifies  <i>yoga</i>  or  the  science  of  the
individual   consciousness   attaining   communion   with    the    ultimate
consciousness and how this relates to equanimity  of  actions.  Without  any
other desires internal or external only the person who relishes  and  revels
in the <i>atma</i> or eternal  soul  is  alone  eligible  for  realisation  of  the
<i>Brahman</i> or the spiritual substratum pervading all existence  and  the  bliss
accompanying it. The communion of the <i>atma</i> is the same as the  communion  of
the <i>Brahman</i> for they both possess the same eternal nature and the  only  way
this communion can be attained is through mediation of  <i>yoga</i>  and  no  other
way.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Now begins the summary.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
So it can be understood that if even by remembrance of the potencies of  the
Supreme Lord so much happiness can be gained then what can be said  for  one
who devotes themselves wholeheartedly to the Supreme Lord in a  concentrated
manner.
<p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Here Lord Krishna describes the reality for the person who desists from
attachments to anything except the atma or eternal soul and who derives all
pleasures exclusively from their internal relationship with the atma
relinquishing all desires for sensuous material enticements and instead
delighting in brahma-yoga or the science of the individual consciousness
attaining communion with the Ultimate Consciousness by realising the
spiritual substratum pervading all existence. Such a person attains sukham-
aksayam or imperishable happiness. Next it will be illustrated how desire
for material sense gratification can be easily abandoned.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
If a person is inclined to attachment to sensual pleasures they  will  never
have  the  opportunity  to  experience  the  transcendental  bliss  of   the
realisation of  the  <i>Brahman</i>  or  the  spiritual  substratum  pervading  all
existence. But a question may be raised what happiness can a  person  derive
from life if they have introverted their senses and  are  averted  to  sense
objects. Lord Krishna  states  the  compound  words  <i>sukham-aksayam</i>  meaning
unlimited happiness is what such persons attains for with  disconnection  to
the senses and objects of the  senses  a  natural  detachment  arises  which
frees one from worldly desires allowing one to focus within on  the  eternal
<i>atma</i> or  eternal  soul  where  one  tastes  boundless  joy  and  experiences
unlimited bliss.  In the moksadharma section of the  Mahabharata  it  states
that: The pleasures of the senses in this world and  the  joys  of  heavenly
pleasures cannot be compared  to  even  1/16th  part  of  the  pleasure  one
derives from renouncing the desire for material  sense  gratification.  This
is the essential component that paves the way for perceiving the <i>Brahman</i>  or
spiritual substratum pervading all existence, <i>atma tattva</i> or realisation  of
the soul and <i>moksa</i> or liberation from the material existence  and  cognition
of the ultimate truth of the Supreme Being.
<p>
