<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
Lord Krishna speaks this verse with a view to  instruct  how  the  preceding
practice is  based  on  the  sacred  syllable  <i>OM</i>  which  is  the  personal,
transcendental sound vibration of the Supreme Lord. The utilisation  of  the
sacred <i>OM</i> mantra is a more direct means than the mere practice of  directing
the life force to the proper place in the physical body.  The  word  <i>aksaram</i>
means  imperishable.  The  imperishable  condition  that  adepts  of   Vedic
scriptures describe  as  in  the  Brihadaranya  Upanisad  III.VIII.IX  which
states: Under the mighty control of this imperishable the sun and  the  moon
are held coursing in  their  positions.  Which  aspirants  is  Lord  Krishna
referring to? Those aspirants who are  striving,  who  have  eradicated  all
desires, who have dedicated themselves to celibacy and  the  abstinent  life
with their spiritual preceptor. These aspirants are qualified to attain  the
ultimate goal. Lord  Krishna  speaks  the  word  <i>sangrahena</i>  or  in  summary
meaning He will explain these things in brief revealing how to attain them.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The ultimate goal to be attained  is  explained  by  Lord  Krishna  in  this
verse. Since the aspirants attain that consciousness it is  known  as  <i>padam</i>
or state and once that state  is  reached  it  is  designated  by  the  word
<i>visanti</i> meaning to enter. The Narada Purana states: That the  ultimate  goal
to be attained is Lord Krishna is confirmed in the Vedic  scriptures.  Since
that consciousness is achieved by the ascetics, it is known as <i>padam.</i>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Now begins the summation.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<i>Brahmacarya</i> or voluntary celibacy for spiritual  purposes  denotes  attuning
one's  consciousness  in  full  devotion  to  the  Supreme  Lord   excluding
everything else.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Now Lord Krishna explains the other  <i>padam</i>  or  state  which  is  for  those
aspirants who wish for <i>atma-tattva</i>  or  realisation  of  the  soul  and  the
secure and permanent  destination  of  <i>moksa</i>  or  liberation  from  material
existence. This state  is  <i>aksaram</i>  or  imperishable.  In  the  Brihadaranya
Upanisad V.VIII.VIII beginning <i>sahovacha-itad vai  tad</i>  explains  that  this
state is not material. It is that which the ascetics of the  worlds  attempt
to gain access to. It is that ardent longing for which men  strictly  follow
the vow of  <i>brahmacaryam</i> or voluntary celibacy.  <i>Padam</i>  is  that  which  one
mentally proceeds to achieve the goal of their endeavours. <i>Padyate</i>  is  non-
different from <i>gamyate</i> meaning that  which  is  followed  being  the  method
adopted by the mind. The purport is that Lord  Krishna  is  giving  a  brief
description of the methodology by which  an  aspirant  for  <i>atma-tattva</i>  and
<i>moksa</i>  must  meditate  on  Him  to  achieve  the  ultimate  goal  they  have
envisioned. Meditation upon  Lord  Krishna  in  His  aspect  of  <i>aksaram</i>  or
imperishable is described in the Brihadaranya Upanisad V.VIII.VIII-XI.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
After presenting the normal practice of meditation Lord  Krishna  introduces
the topmost method designated  by  the  word  <i>aksaram</i>  meaning  imperishable
which He promises to reveal in summary  how  to  attain  this  imperishable,
ultimate goal and  that  once  it  is  attained  the  Supreme  Lord  who  is
<i>saccidananda</i> or the embodiment of eternal truth, knowledge and  bliss  which
the Vaisnavas and Brahmins who are knowers of the Vedic scriptures  describe
as imperishable and indestructible. The Brhadaranyaka III.IX.VIII  states:To
this state the Brahmins attribute the term indestructible to that  which  is
not material and that  which  is  not  subtle,  etc.  This  state  is  where
determined ascetics free from passion and desire for  sense  objects  strive
to enter. This is the state where  the  enlightened  ascetics  perceive  the
experience of  the  <i>atma</i>  or  soul  as  omnipresent  and  omniscient.  Their
consciousness resides in the highest  substratum  and  they  become  totally
detached from  contact  with  three  dimensional  material  nature  and  are
incapable as such of performing any action that is in  separation  from  the
Supreme Lord. They becoming His own, remain in this state adhering  to  Him,
taking  full  shelter  in  Him,  desiring  to  know  that  for   which   the
<i>brahmacarya's</i> or voluntary celibates remain firm in their vow.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 8, verse 11 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
