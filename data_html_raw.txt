<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna being the universal self is being declared in  this  verse  and
the next three. He states He is <i>Kratu</i> or Vedic rituals such as <i>agnistoma</i>  or
fire offerings<i>.</i> He is also <i>Yagna</i> or offerings of propitiation prescribed  in
the Vedic scriptures known as <i>Smritis</i> or those books  of  Vedic  injunctions
and knowledge which has been remembered and recorded throughout history.  He
is <i>sraddha</i> or the oblations to the departed ancestors.  He  is  the  healing
potency within  medicinal herbs in the forests. He is the <i>mantra</i>  or  sacred
Vedic invocations recited in exact meter of great  potency  uttered  at  the
commencement of any Vedic ritual and during its performance. He is also  the
<i>ghee</i> or clarified butter extracted exclusively from the milk  of  cow  which
being offered into the fire validates the right and gives the  means  for  a
<i>yagna</i> to be performed. So He is verily the fire, the offering put  into  the
fire and the <i>yagna</i> as well.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
So in this verse Lord Krishna describes some  of  His  qualities  concerning
Vedic rituals.
<p>
<p>
He is <i>Kratu</i> or Vedic rituals like Jyotishtoma  which  awards  <i>Swarga</i>  the
heavenly planets
<p>
<p>
He is <i>Yagna</i> or the act  of  daily  propitiation  enjoined  in  the  Vedic
scriptures
<p>
<p>
He is <i>Sraddha</i>  the food oblations offered to the departed  ancestors  and
presiding deities
<p>
<p>
He is <i>ausadham</i> the potency within the healing medicinal herb
<p>
<p>
He is the <i>mantra</i> or the sacred verses chanted at the performance  of  all
Vedic rituals
<p>
<p>
He is <i>ajam</i> or the <i>ghee</i> which is clarified  butter  extracted  exclusively
from cows milk.
<p>
<p>
He is the fire known as <i>Ahavaniyas</i> the sacrificial fire ignited from  the
consecrated fire.
<p>
<p>
He is <i>hutam</i> denoting <i>homan</i> or the oblations offered into the fire and the
ritual itself.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna elaborates on the many aspects of His diversity in  this  verse
and the next three. He states that He is <i>Kratu</i> or Vedic  rituals,  <i>Yagna</i>  or
Vedic offerings, <i>Sraddha</i> or  Vedic  oblations  to  the  departed  ancestors,
<i>asudham</i> or the potency of the medicinal healing  herb,  the  <i>mantra</i>  or  the
sacred Sanskrit chant of invocation recited by duly bonafide  Brahmins  from
one of the four authorised <i>sampradayas</i> or lines of disciplic succession.  He
is also <i>aijam</i> or <i>ghee</i> which is clarified butter  exclusively  from  the  cow
which without no Vedic <i>yagna</i> can  be  authentically  performed.  He  is  the
<i>Ahavaniya</i> fire which is ignited from the consecrated fire and He is the  act
of offering the oblations into the fire and the ritual itself.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 9, verse 16 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
