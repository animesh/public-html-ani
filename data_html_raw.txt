<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The binding nature of <i>rajas</i> or mode of passion and its  characteristics  are
now stated by Lord Krishna. It should be understood that <i>rajas</i> is  the  root
of sense gratification and lust. Therefore <i>rajas</i> is  the  source  of  desire
and attachment. Desire is hankering for that which is yet to be  experienced
and attachment is the obsession to have  that  which  has  been  experienced
already. By  instigating  and  increasing  the  propensity  to  continuously
perform actions <i>rajas</i> binds the <i>jiva</i> or embodied being  tightly  in  <i>samsara</i>
the perpetual cycle of birth and death due to accute desire  and  attachment
to enjoy apparent and inapparent results.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Because of attraction the attachment to craving binds by becoming  incessant
and insatiable.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna explains that the mode of passion is known as <i>rajas</i> and is  the
cause of sexual desires known as carnal  lust.  From  <i>rajas</i>  arises  <i>trishna</i>
which is desire for all forms of sensual enjoyments through  the  medium  of
the senses such as seeing, hearing,  tasting,  smelling  and  feeling.  Also
arising from <i>rajas</i> is <i>sanga</i> or desire to be in the  association  of  family,
friends  and  loved  ones.  Thus  <i>rajas</i>  encourages  desires  and   promotes
activities to enjoy such desires which binds one to the reactions of  merits
or demerits and keeps the <i>jiva</i> or embodied being  incessantly  revolving  in
<i>samsara</i> or the perpetual cycle of  birth  and  death.  This  propensity  for
constantly performing actions is inherent and also causes the <i>jiva</i>  to  take
birth at a specific place with a  particular  body  suitable  to  enjoy  the
rewards of previous actions. Hence by instigating the desire for  performing
works one is kept in bondage. In this way <i>rajas</i> is  known  to  be  the  root
cause of lust, sensual desires and attachment.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Here Lord Krishna explains <i>raja guna</i> or the mode of  passion  and  describes
how it binds the <i>jiva</i> or embodied being to material existence.  The  quality
of passion gives the impetus to perform activities for the desire  of  sense
gratification and identifying in the mind such sense objects to  be  enjoyed
by the senses. Desire is the hankering for the acquisition of objects to  be
acquired and attachment is obsession for  objects  that  have  already  been
acquired. This <i>rajas</i> tightly binds the <i>jiva</i> through incessant  readiness  to
perform actions to satisfy desires and attachment to the lure  of  perceived
and unperceived results for such actions.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 14, verse 7 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
