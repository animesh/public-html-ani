<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
One who has yoked themselves to the  consciousness  of  being  the  doer  of
their actions cannot help  being  contaminated  by  the  reactions  to  such
actions and on account of this their mind remains impure and hence they  are
far adrift from renunciation and are in a  grave  predicament.  Apprehending
such  a  situation  Lord  Krishna  explains  that  one  who  dedicates   and
consummates all actions as offerings to the Supreme Lord without  attachment
to rewards is not affected by sinful reactions which are most abominable  on
account of their binding power to material nature
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Whosoever is properly situated in the state of equanimity  is  not  affected
by reactions due  to  <i>karma  yoga</i>  or  the  prescribed  Vedic  actions  they
perform. This means one does not desire the rewards  of  their  actions  but
instead dutifully offers the results of all their actions unto  the  Supreme
Lord.  For  the  purpose  of  emphasising  the  method  of  performing  this
spiritual  practice  and  for  the  sake  of  removing  any  impression   of
casualness the subject  of  renouncing  the  rewards  of  one's  actions  is
reiterated again and again by Lord Krishna.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The word <i>brahmany</i> used here is referring to the  Supreme  Lord  who  is  the
antithesis to material matter. One who performs  all  activities  reflecting
that they relate to the  functions  of  the  body  and  senses  and  pertain
exclusively to material matter and who knows that in reality the  individual
consciousness  has  nothing  to  do   with   it   remains   unaffected   and
uncontaminated from sinful reactions  arising  from  misconceptions  of  the
physical body being the <i>atma</i> or soul and the subsequent erroneous  mentality
that causes bondage to <i>samsara</i> or the endless cycle of birth  and  death  in
the material existence.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The question may arise that a person possessing the characteristics  of  the
previous verse with a pure heart and controlled senses may be unaffected  by
reactions to actions; but how can those without such characteristics  become
unattached to desire for the rewards of actions. Lord Krishna  answers  this
by instructing everyone to offer and dedicate all  actions  to  the  Supreme
Lord. The desire for rewards of actions is the fundamental  reason  for  all
ego conceptions of being the physical body and the root cause of <i>samsara</i>  or
bondage in the cycle of birth and death which so viciously  apprehends  one.
So desire for rewards should  be immediately be given up so one will not  be
contaminated by sinful reactions.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 5, verse 10 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
