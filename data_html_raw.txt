<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
In order to ascertain exactly what  is  the  final  conclusion  of  all  the
teachings and instructions  that  have  been  imparted  up  until  now;  the
Supreme Lord Krishna concisely summarises the entire Bhagavad-Gita  in  this
concluding  chapter  by  clearly  distinguishing  the   difference   between
renunciation of actions caused by the impulses of  desire  and  renunciation
of the desire for rewards for  one's  actions.  In  previous  chapters  Lord
Krishna has elaborated on the mental renunciation of all actions in  chapter
5, verse 13  as  well  as  renunciation  of  actions  through  <i>yoga</i>  or  the
individual   consciousness   attaining   communion   with    the    ultimate
consciousness in chapter 9, verse  28.  He  has  also  explained  renouncing
attachment to actions while renouncing their rewards in chapter 4, verse  20
and likewise explained  the  renunciation  of  the  rewards  resulting  from
actions  in  chapter  12,  verse  11.  It  should  be  understood  that  the
infinitely merciful  and  totally  omniscient  Supreme  Lord  Krishna  never
teaches or exemplifies contradictory knowledge. Whatever  and  wherever  His
divine <i>lila</i> or pastimes are manifesting they are always perfection  and  the
epitome of <i>dharma</i> or eternal righteousness.  Everything He does or  expounds
upon whether instructional or by example is  always  fully  harmonious  with
the Vedas and in completely complimentary to  the  conclusions  of  all  the
Vedic scriptures. So to distinctly know the difference between  renunciation
of performing actions and to reconcile  it  harmoniously  with  renunciation
for the  results  of  actions  is  the  poignant  and  penetrating  question
requested to be answered here.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Hari OM! In this final chapter the Supreme Lord Krishna summarises in  brief
all of the perennial principles and eternal truths that  were  presented  in
the previous 17 chapters and establishes the collective  conclusion  to  all
of them.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The preceding chapters 16 and 17 elaborated the following subjects  by  Lord
Krishna:
1) The only means of achieving the four <i>purusarthas</i>  or  goals  of  human
existence which are <i>kama</i> or  pleasure,  <i>artha</i>  or  wealth<i>,  dharma</i>  or
righteousness and <i>moksa</i> or liberation from material existence which is
the quintessence of them all is to adhere to and follow the ordinances
and injunctions of the Vedic scriptures by the performance of <i>yagna</i> or
ritualistic propitiation and worship  to  the  Supreme  Lord  Krishna,
<i>tapah</i> or austerities and penance authorised in  the  Vedic  scriptures
and <i>danam</i> or charity to the Vaisnava Brahmins from  one  of  the  four
bonafide sampradayas as revealed in Vedic scriptures.
2) That all Vedic rituals and observances  are  always  predicated  first
with the <i>pranava</i> OM the transcendental sound vibration of the  Supreme
Lord denoting the first breath making this reverberating hum which the
Sanskrit root is <i>pranu.</i>
3)  The  distinction  of  that  representing  the  <i>brahman</i>  or  spiritual
substratum pervading all existence and leading to <i>moksa</i> is  symbolised
by TAT and that  representing  <i>prakriti</i>  or  the  material  substratum
pervading physical existence  confering  <i>kama,  artha</i>  and  <i>dharma</i>  is
symbolised by SAT.
4) That <i>yagna</i> or ritualistic propitiation and worship performed  for  the
satisfaction of the Supreme  Lord  when  devoid  of  any  desires  for
rewards becomes successful
5) That those initiated Vaisnava  brahmanas  performing  <i>yagnas</i>  achieved
their  ordainment as a result of the prominence of <i>sattva guna</i> or mode
of goodness permeating their character enhanced by the sole intake  of
only <i>sattvic</i> foods.
<p>
<p>
In this final chapter Lord Krishna  concisely  delineates  the  following
subject matters:
<i>1) Sannyasa</i> the renunciation of action  and  <i>tyaja</i>  the  renunciation  of
actions rewards
2) The exact nature and mood of <i>tyaja.</i>
3) The comprehension that the Supreme Lord Krishna is the repository  and
agency of everything.
4) A description of the effects of the three <i>gunas</i> or modes  of  material
nature  illustrating  that  <i>sattva  guna</i>  alone  leads  to  <i>moksa</i>   or
liberation from material existence and is thus the only <i>guna</i> worthy of
cultivation.
5) How activities  appropriated  in  the  <i>varnas</i>  or  four  caste  system
indicates the natural propensities of a <i>jiva</i> or embodied  being  based
on <i>karma</i> or reactions to past actions are actually authorised acts  of
worship to the Supreme Lord accomplishing His attainment.
6) The quintessential conclusion of the divine discourse Srimad Bhagavad-
Gita is that <i>bhakti</i> or exclusive loving devotion to the  Supreme  Lord
Krishna or any  of  His  authorised  incarnations  and  expansions  as
revealed in Vedic scriptures is the paramount goal of all existence.
<p>
<p>
The question enquired about is the distinct difference  between  <i>sannyasa</i>
and <i>tyaja</i> as well as their similarities. Both  are  subtle  not  easy  to
understand. Both are situated in renunciation and  both  lead  to  <i>moksa.</i>
Literally <i>sannyasa</i> means putting away and literally  <i>tyaja</i>  means  giving
up. <i>Sannyasa</i> expresses abandonment  of  desires  for  actions  and  <i>tyaja</i>
exhibits the abandonment for the rewards of actions. The Mundaka Upanisad
III.II.VI beginning <i>veda ta vijnano sunisch</i> means:  Those  of  restrained
senses who lead a life  of  renunciation  with  Vedic  knowledge  achieve
liberation. Not by action, not by wealth, not  by  progeny  can  this  be
achieved. Those enlightened  <i>jivas</i>  who  have  ascertained  what  is  the
essence of the Vedic scriptures and assimilating it within  their  hearts
by renunciation dissolve all their <i>karmas</i> and achieve <i>moksa</i> and the final
beatitude. In order to illustrate the nature of both  <i>sannyasa</i> and  <i>tyaja</i>
and prove that they are one and the same Lord Krishna first corrects  the
misapprehension that they are different.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
This is the final chapter of Srimad Bhagavad-Gita  and  in  it  the  Supreme
Lord Krishna gives a concise synopsis of all the subjects presented  in  the
previous 17 chapters. This He does in order to  impress  upon  our  memories
the topics  elaborated  upon  earlier  and  to  clearly  delineate  how  all
conceptions harmoniously unite in  the  marvellous  sublime  beauty  of  the
final conclusion. At the end of the  last  chapter  it  was  explained  that
actions were  only  declared  righteous  if  they  were  performed  for  the
exclusive satisfaction of the Supreme Lord Krishna or any of His  authorised
incarnations  revealed  in  Vedic  scriptures.  Such   actions   exclusively
dedicated in chapter 17, verse 27 for His satisfaction alone  are  known  as
SAT and clearly denote that  the  desire  for  rewards  is  what  is  to  be
abandoned and not the activity. This was also confirmed earlier  in  chapter
12, verse 11 where Lord  Krishna  advises  that  controlling  the  mind  one
should relinquish the desire for the rewards of  actions.  Even  earlier  in
chapter 5, verse  13  Lord  Krishna  had  advised  mentally  renouncing  all
actions.
<p>
Why then do these two  instructions  seem  contradictory  and  what  is  the
correct understanding regarding the renunciation  of  all  actions  and  the
renunciation of desire for the receiving the rewards of  actions?  The  true
nature of abandonment of actions and  the  abandonment  of  the  desire  for
rewards and the distinction between  the  two  is  what  Lord  Krishna  will
address. In this context it must be taken into consideration whether or  not
<i>sannyasa</i> or renunciation and <i>tyaja</i> or abandonment  are  actually  different.
The Mundaka Upanisad III.II.VI beginning <i>veda tat vijnano  sunisch</i>  meaning:
Those  who  have  purified  themselves   through   renunciation   and   have
ascertained and comprehended the Vedic  scriptures  will  achieve  <i>moksa</i>  or
liberation from material existence. Neither  through  actions,  nor  wealth,
nor progeny but only by renunciation is immortality gained.
<p>
The  evocatives  <i>mahabaho</i>  meaning  mighty  armed  denotes  Lord   Krishna's
indomitable might and <i>kesinisudana</i> refers to His destroying a mighty  demon.
So He is requested to by His supreme power to destroy  the  misunderstanding
of renunciation along with destroying the enemies on  the  battlefield.  The
evocative <i>Hrsikesa</i> acknowledges that Lord Krishna is the  inner  monitor  of
the mind and that only He can remove all doubts.
<p>
