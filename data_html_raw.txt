<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Lord Krishna now discloses the results of  first  the  divine  nature  which
qualifies for higher spiritual knowledge  leading  to  <i>moksa</i>  or  liberation
from material existence and secondly He discloses the demoniac nature  which
disqualifies  one  from  <i>moksa</i>  and  insures  enslavement  in  <i>samsara</i>   the
perpetual cycle of  birth  and  death.  Lord  Krishna  perceiving  that  His
devotee was distraught by the idea that he may not be  a  fit  aspirant  for
<i>moksa</i> and hence subject to <i>samsara</i>, the Supreme Lord consoles him  with  the
words <i>ma sucah</i> meaning not to worry, assuring him that he is  definitely  of
the divine nature.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna encourages His devotee to grieve not or be  despondent  at  the
thought that he may not be qualified enough, for without a doubt he is  born
of the divine nature.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna explains the opposing results of  the  divine  nature  and  the
demoniac  nature.  The  divine  nature  which  follows  the  ordinances  and
injunctions of the Vedic scriptures has been regarded from  time  immemorial
by self-realised sages and rishis as conducive to <i>moksa</i> or  liberation  from
material existence due to spiritual knowledge and attaining  communion  with
the Supreme Lord. The demoniac nature leads one to  act  contrarily  to  the
ordinances and injunctions of the Vedic scriptures and hence  are  conducive
to enslavement in <i>samsara</i>  the  perpetual  cycle  of  birth  and  death  and
bondage in material existence taking birth  each  time  in  lower  and  more
degraded life forms. Seeing despondency on His devotees face,  Lord  Krishna
assures him not to worry for he is  without  a  doubt  born  of  the  divine
nature.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 16, verse 5 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
