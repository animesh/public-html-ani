<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Endued with purified intelligence of a nature  secured  in  <i>sattva  guna</i>  or
mode of  goodness,  tenacious  control  of  the  mind  insuring  that  one's
purified intelligence is constant and steady. Relinquishing all desires  for
sense objects  and  abandoning  the  ever  fickle  dualities  of  likes  and
dislikes such a one  is  qualified  to  realise  the  <i>brahman</i>  or  spiritual
substratum  pervading  all  existence.  Such  an  aspirant  will   sequester
themselves  in  a  pure,  remote  location  such  as  forest  or   mountain.
Restraining speech, the impulses of the mind and the impetus  of  the  body,
always devoted to the practice of <i>yoga</i> or facilitating  communion  with  the
Supreme Lord which comes from reflection, contemplation and meditation  upon
the Supreme Lord Krishna constantly striving in  this  endeavour  with  firm
dispassion so that it remains constant and unbroken.  Rejecting  the  egoism
of the delusion that  one  is  the  doer  and  controller  which  gives  the
illusion that one is free from  worldly  attachment.  Rejecting  all  things
superfluous which are inclined to lead one  away  from  spiritual  pursuits.
Rejection of objects of the senses and the desire  to  enjoy  them  even  if
they appear unsolicited. Thus one who has steadfastly arrived at this  state
has become totally tranquil and serene having achieved   supreme  peace  and
such a one is qualified to realise the <i>brahman.</i>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna is stating that by imbibing and  actualising  these  attributes
one assumes the nature of <i>brahman</i> the  spiritual  substratum  pervading  all
existence.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<i>Buddhi</i>  is  spiritual  intelligence  the  consciousness  which   exclusively
focuses on the <i>atma</i>.
<i>Manas</i> refers to the mind, senses  and  body  which  acting  in  such  a  way
discourages desires for sense gratification  and  encourages  reflection  on
the Supreme Lord. <i>Vairagyam-samipasritah</i> means renouncing the objects  which
the senses hanker for and indifference to  mundane  pursuits.  It  is  while
chasing such pursuits that occasionally friendship or enmity  transpires  so
ceasing from these pursuits would free one from <i>raga-dvesau</i>  attraction  and
repulsion. <i>Vivikta-sevi</i> is seeking solitude  in  remote  places  aloof  from
disturbances to meditation.  Moderation  in  eating  and  sleeping.  <i>Dhyana-</i>
<i>yoga</i> means internally  performing  continuous  <i>bhakti</i>  or  exclusive  loving
devotional service unto the Supreme Lord Krishna or any of  His  <i>avatars</i>  or
incarnations and expansions as authorised  in  Vedic  scriptures.  Gradually
increasing aversion towards anything that is not connected to  the  <i>atma</i>  or
immortal soul and the Supreme Lord. <i>Ahankaram</i> is egoistic  conceptions  that
considers the body to be all in all and cannot discern  that  they  are  not
their physical body. <i>Balam</i> is lust for power, <i>darpan</i> is vain  conceit  which
is derived from it and <i>nirmanam</i> is the notion  one  possesses  what  is  not
one's own. <i>Santah</i> is  tranquillity   peacefulness,  the  serenity  of  bliss
acquired by  constant  reflection  and  meditation  upon  the  Supreme  Lord
qualifies one to achieve realisation of the <i>brahman</i> or spiritual  substratum
pervading all existence and and achieving <i>moksa</i> or liberation from  material
existence attains <i>atma tattva</i> or soul realisation and enters into the  bliss
of communion with the Supreme Lord.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord  Krishna  explains  that  one   endowed   with   spiritually   purified
intelligence  is  naturally  predominated  with  <i>sattva  guna</i>  the  mode  of
goodness.  The  classification  of  higher  and  lower  living  entities  is
determined by the degree in which the mind is controlled and the senses  are
restrained. The mind is controlled by not indulging in positive or  negative
thoughts and  illusory  conceptions  and  the  control  of  the  senses  is
neutrality  towards  them  without  aversion  or  attraction  towards  their
objects. By living in pure and remote place, exercising a light,  vegetarian
diet, moderation of speech, mind and body, three times daily  meditating  on
the spiritual master and the Supreme Lord morning, noon and evening in  full
dispassion, completely eradicating all egoism and conceptions  that  one  is
the  physical  body.  Lack  of  craving  bodily  sense   gratification   and
soliciting  pleasure  of  the  senses,   absence   of   amassing   excessive
possessions and freedom from mental disturbances  bestows  the  tranquillity
and peace of  mind  to  qualify  an  aspirant  to  realise  the  <i>brahman</i>  or
spiritual substratum pervading  al  existence  which  is  transcendental  to
material  nature  and  situated  on  the  spiritual  platform  is  pure  and
impeccable without any defect or blemish.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 18, verse 51 , 52 , 53 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
