<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Lord Krishna continuing this theme now explains that one who  is  completely
free from desire does not become  tainted  by  the  disease  of  materialism
while performing the natural functions that occur  automatically  to  insure
bodily maintenance. Another interpretation of this  verse  for  one  who  is
already situated in <i>jnana yoga</i> or the cultivation of knowledge and  is  free
from desires to enjoy and hankering for rewards  and  has  relinquished  all
possessions as well. Such a person who performs  natural  activities  for  a
renunciate like <i>biksa</i> or begging alms from householders  at  meal  times  to
maintain their existence or prescribed activities such as fasting  from  all
grains on <i>Ekadasi</i> which is the 11th day of the waxing and waning moon.  Such
a person is not implicated by  the  disease  of  materialism  by  what  they
perform.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Lord Krishna now gives the method of renouncing desires.  Restraining  one's
mind and body by the conscious of the <i>atma</i> or  soul  which  is  referred  to
here denotes not being dependent. By renunciation of the senses one  becomes
devoid of pride. One who is convinced that the  Supreme  Lord  performs  all
actions and  that  they  do  not  perform  any  actions  is  free  from  all
reactions.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Here Lord Krishna uses the  word  <i>nirasir</i>  means  bereft  of  expectancy  or
devoid of all desires for rewards. The words <i>yata-cittatma</i> means to  control
the mind by the power of the <i>atma</i> or soul, keeping  the  mind  tranquil  and
equiposed, free from  agitation.  The  words  <i>tyakta-sarva-parigrahah</i>  means
abandoning all cravings for sense objects and sense pleasures.  As  long  as
one has life one should perform all actions as a matter of duty merely as  a
function of their body; in this way there are no reactions  to  actions  and
no disease is incurred. One will be free of this. The disease is <i>samsara</i>  or
repetitive bondage of birth and death in the material  existence.  If  <i>karma</i>
<i>yoga</i> or prescribed Vedic activities are  performed  in  this  way  by  those
seeking <i>moksa</i> or liberation  from  <i>samsara</i>  this  in  itself  is  sufficient
enough to lead one to <i>atma tattva</i> or realisation of  the  soul,  eliminating
the necessity of having to engage in the  intermediate  step  of  performing
<i>jnana yoga</i> or the cultivation of Vedic  knowledge  by  strictly  controlling
the senses and the cessation of actions.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
On who has so renounced desire and hankerings and has controlled  senses  as
well, even  if  they  perform  some  activity  to  maintain  natural  bodily
management they are exempt from  being  implicated  into  pious  and  sinful
reactions. This is Lord Krishna's meaning.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 4, verse 21 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
