<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The Supreme Lord Krishna known as <i>Parabrahma</i> the source of  the  <i>brahman</i>  or
spiritual substratum pervading all existence, is the light  of  all  lights,
the illuminator of even the sun and the stars. He is shining,  resplendently
effulgent all things shine from Him, by His light all creation  is  full  of
light.  Therefore  <i>Parabrahma</i>  is  intouched  by  darkness  and  beyond  all
nescience  untouched  by  ignorance.  The  Svetasvatara  Upanisad   III.VII.
beginning <i>veda  hametam  purusam</i>  meaning:  The  Supreme  Lord  shines  more
brilliantly then the sun and by realising Him  only  one  attains  <i>moksa</i>  or
liberation there is no other way to accomplish this. It  is  this  knowledge
that is to manifest in the functions  of  the  intellect  regarding  the  20
superlative virtues given in verses 8 to 12. It  is  also  knowable  in  the
form of sound by <i>OM</i> the primordial sound frequency of the Supreme  Lord.  It
is also accessible through spiritual  practices  and  easily  attainable  by
<i>bhakti</i> or exclusive loving devotion to the Supreme Lord.  The  discovery  of
the <i>atma</i> or immortal soul within the etheric heart is  being  emphasised  by
acquiring knowledge and performing appropriately for self- realisation.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Since the  Supreme  Lord  Krishna  is  achieved  by  experiencing  spiritual
knowledge, He is understood to be the goal of all knowledge. Know  the  <i>atma</i>
or immortal soul by experiencing the  <i>atma</i>.  As  the  Supreme  Lord  is  the
bestower of the <i>atma</i> and the <i>atma</i> is only knowable through Him  then  He  is
the most worthy object of knowledge. If the  Supreme  Lord  is  knowable  by
other means then the words He spoke earlier  in  verse  13  <i>jneyam  yat  tat</i>
meaning all that which is worth knowing would make  this  verse  repetitive.
Now He affirms here the means to achieve what He revealed  as  the  goal  in
verse 13 negating any contradiction. The Supreme Lord knows Himself  by  His
Self alone. His knowing is not dependent on any external source  outside  of
Himself. In special cases He is  able  to  be  known  by  others  as  it  is
possible to know Him by His desire and also by realization of the <i>atma.</i>  But
only by His grace can this manifest and without His grace knowledge  of  Him
can never manifest. The  Supreme  Lord  is  self  illuminated,  He  is  self
contented and He is equal towards all. Therefore as He is  the  foremost  of
all, He is the object of attainment  for  all  and  all  <i>jivas</i>  or  embodied
beings should seek His grace for only by His grace can He be known.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Lights are those found in the sun, the stars, fire, gems, etc. The light  of
these lights denotes that which illuminates all these or that by which  they
are perceived denoting the light of consciousness by  which  all  luminaries
such as the sun and the stars are seen to shine. As for  human  made  lights
such as  lamps,  torches  and  the  rest,  they  only  dispel  the  darkness
intervening  between  the  sense  of  sight  and  its   object   and   their
illuminating power is limited to the sense perception of  sight.  But  there
is no limitation with the light and sight of  the  <i>atma</i>  once  it  has  been
realised. The words <i>tamasah param</i> means beyond  darkness  referring  to  the
darkness  of  <i>tamas</i>  or  ignorance  designating  the  subtle   but   limited
conditions  of  <i>prakriti</i>  or  the  material  substratum  pervading  physical
existence; but the <i>atma</i> or immortal soul being eternal is transcendental  to
any conditions or limitations.  The  words  <i>jnanam  jneyam</i>  means  knowledge
worthy of being known comprehensible by the intellect and  characterised  by
consciousness. The words <i>jnana  gamyam</i>    means  wisdom  accessible  through
knowledge or that wisdom gained from embodying and  living  the  20  virtues
given in verses 8 through 12 beginning with <i>amanitvam</i>  or  reverence.  These
exalted virtues which  constitute  spiritual  knowledge  are  the  means  to
achieve the wisdom of the <i>atma</i>. As the <i>atma</i> is situated within the heart  of
all sentient beings, the Supreme Lord Krishna known as  <i>Parabrahma</i>  is  <i>hrdi</i>
<i>sarvasy dhisthitam</i> situated in the heart of all sentient beings.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The Supreme Lord Krishna known as <i>Parabrahma</i> is the source  of  the  <i>brahman</i>
or  spiritual  substratum  pervading  all  existence.   It  is  solely   His
effulgence that illuminates all luminaries  such  as  the  sun,  the  stars,
lightning, jewels,  etc.  with  light.  These  luminaries  only  dispel  the
darkness hindering the vision between the eyes  and  their  objects  whereas
the knowledge of a mere  spark  of  the  Supreme  Lord  being  the  <i>atma</i>  or
immortal  soul  illuminates  all  physical  lights  and  without  the   <i>atma</i>
knowledge of illumination is not possible. That is why the <i>atma</i> is  said  to
be totally beyond the darkness of <i>maya</i> which  is   the  deluding,  external,
illusory potency of the Supreme Lord. The illusory nature of  <i>maya</i>  is  ever
changing by nature whereas the <i>atma</i> is eternal and unchanging. The  <i>atma</i>  is
itself  knowledge as well as the goal of all knowledge and  is  most  worthy
of attaining by the acquisition of knowledge. The qualifications  to  obtain
this knowledge is found in the 20 excellent  virtues  expounded  earlier  in
verse 8 to 12 beginning with reverence,  humility  etc.  which  are  in  the
hearts  of  all  human  beings  albeit  dormant   or   active.   The   words
<i>hrdi</i> <i>sarvasya  dhisthitam</i>  emphasises  that  the  Supreme  Lord  Krishna  is
specially situated in the etheric heart of all living entities.
<p>
