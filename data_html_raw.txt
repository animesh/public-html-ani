<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The predominating effect of <i>sattva guna</i> or  mode  of  goodness  is  that  it
instils in the <i>jiva</i> or embodied being  the  attraction  for  happiness  even
though the seeds of misery and sorrow are also inherent. So <i>sattva  guna</i>  by
such natural but strong attraction for happiness binds the <i>jiva</i> to  material
existence and <i>raja guna</i> the mode of  passion  binds  one  by  the  work  and
effort constantly undertaken to achieve such  happiness  and  <i>tama  guna</i>  or
mode of ignorance binds one to material  existence  by  distorted  delusions
and perverted perceptions of knowledge even if heard from liberated  beings.
<i>Tamas</i>  also manifests as neglecting the teachings of great liberated  beings
or by acting contrary to these   teachings  because  such  teachings  differ
from ones personal understanding. The  word  <i>uta</i>  means  and  so  forth  and
indicates that  <i>tamas</i>  binds  one  as  well  to  lethargy  listlessness  and
laziness.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><font size=2>There is no commentary for this verse.</font>
</td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The cardinal features of  three  <i>gunas</i>  or  modes  of  <i>sattva</i>  or  goodness<i>,</i>
<i>rajas</i> or passion and <i>tamas</i> or nescience are now  being  delineated  by  Lord
Krishna. The main quality of <i>sattva</i> is its ability  to  confer  blessedness.
The main proponent of <i>rajas</i> is that it impels  vigourous  activity  and  the
dominant  factor  in  <i>tamas</i>  is  that  it  obscures  intelligence  giving  a
perverted  view  of  reality  which  results  in  the  tendency  to  perform
unnatural activities. The three <i>gunas</i> are the natural  effects  of  <i>prakriti</i>
or the material substratum pervading physical existence and  constitute  the
qualities of all matter which manifests into physical bodies both gross  and
subtle. How they each give rise to consequences so radically  different  and
conflicting with each other is answered in the next verse.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Here Lord Krishna describes in brief the functional capacity  of  the  three
<i>gunas</i> which are  <i>sattva guna</i> or the mode of goodness, <i>raja guna</i> or the  mode
of passion and <i>tama guna</i> or the mode of ignorance.  The  quality  of  <i>sattva</i>
impels the mind  to  be  attracted  to  happiness.  Although  there  may  be
situations of pain and discomfort the overall tendency of  the  mind  is  to
seek knowledge and experience joy. The quality of <i>rajas</i> activates  the  mind
to be attracted to action. Although there may be situations  of  tranquility
and calm the overall tendency is constant activity. The quality of <i>tamas</i>  is
obscuring and distorting knowledge  even  if  instructed  by  the  spiritual
master. Such  perversion  causes  the  <i>jiva</i>  or  embodied  being  to  commit
degenerative activities that hinder it's well  being  and  blocks  it's  own
best interests.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 14, verse 9 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
