<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Lord Krishna now gives the evidence for His avyakta or unmanifest being
aksara or imperishable having neither birth or destruction and impossible
to be perceived by the mind and senses. The Mundaka Upanisad I.I.VII
states: Similarly from the aksara this creation comes into being. The
aksara is declared to be paramam gatim the supreme goal of human existence
and the ultimate destination. The Katha Upanisad III.XI states: There is
nothing higher than the Purusha. He is the ultimate reality and the paramam
gatim. The proof of it being the supreme goal is verified by the words na
nivarttante meaning one never returns again to the material worldsonce they
attain Lord Krishna's supreme dhama or abode. The six causative case by the
word mama meaning my is used in the sense of identity as when one states
Rahu's head which is all that remains of Rahu, it means only that.
Therefore Lord Krishna, Himself is the paramam gatim and no other. This is
the purport.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The avyakta the unmanifest and aksara the indestructible is the resplendent
Supreme Lord, Krishna Himself, upon whom attaining there is no more
returning to samsara the perpetual cycle of birth and death. The Garuda
Purana states: The avyakta is the Supreme Lord Himself. It shows the use of
the word dhama or abode as also denoting the resplendent form of the
Supreme Lord. Showing that the resplendent Supreme Lord's form and abode
are both indicated such are the confidential meanings understood by those
of spiritual wisdom.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Lord Krishna now reveals His superior conscious <i>avyakta</i> or unmanifest  which
is different in principle and substance  then  the  unconscious  <i>avyakta</i>  or
unmanifest of Brahma which is non-intelligent and operates according to  set
parameters. Lord Krishna's superior <i>avyakta</i> is characterised  by  eternality
due to its possession <i>atma tattva</i> or  soul  realisation.  Thus  it  is  also
characterised  by  <i>jnana</i>  or  consciousness.  <i>Avyakta</i>  is  also   known   as
indistinct because it beyond any perceptive faculty of the  mind  or  senses
to cognise it as a perceivable reality. The purport is  that  <i>avyakta</i>  is  a
principle of self-consciousness and as such  is  completely  unique  in  its
nature. The word <i>sanatanah</i> meaning eternal because His supreior  <i>avyakta</i>  is
not subject to combination and aggregation or resolution and  disintegration
and never disperses or dissipates when all the material elements  of  earth,
water, fire, air and ether in there rudimental forms  and  derivative  forms
dissolve away although His superior <i>avyakta</i> abides within them. This is  why
the Vedic scriptures call it <i>avyakta</i> or indistinct  as  well  as  <i>aksara</i>  or
indestructible. Those steeped in the wisdom of  the  Vedic  scriptures  have
declared that this is the <i>paramam  gatim</i>  or  supreme  exalted  goal  to  be
achieved. Lord Krishna previously mentioned <i>aksara</i> in verse  three  of  this
chapter and will later mention it again in chapters XII.III and XV.XVI.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The superior state of <i>avyakta</i> is  where  <i>atma  tattva</i>  or  soul  realisation
abounds and when once reached immediately precludes forever  the  subjection
to union with matter again as there is no  more  rebirth  for  reincarnation
has been terminated. Lord  Krishna  specifies  their  destination  with  the
words <i>dhama paramam mama</i> meaning His supreme  personal  abode  of  eternity,
knowledge and bliss where all things reciprocate fully with Him and  is  the
abode of the liberated beings. The word <i>dhama</i> also denotes luminosity as  in
the light of consciousness which is the primary attribute  of  the  <i>atma</i>  or
soul. Thus Lord Krishna is indicating His  <i>paramam  dhama</i>  as  non-different
from the infinite consciousness of the  <i>atma</i>  in  contrast  to  the  limited
state of consciousness one possesses who is oblivious to  the  <i>atma</i>  due  to
being deluded by the illusory material energy known as <i>maya</i>  and  cherishing
the association of the senses with sense objects<i>.</i>  The other  <i>avyakta</i>  where
resides <i>praktiti</i> or  the  material  substratum  which  contains  all  living
entities and which is perishable is  controlled  by  Lord  Krishna  as  well
through His manifestation as the eternal <i>atma</i> or  soul  within  the  etheric
heart of all created beings throughout all existence. This is the  abode  of
the non-liberated beings. From the superior <i>avyakta</i> there is  no  return  to
<i>samsara</i> the cycle of birth and death. The next verse will show how the  goal
of the <i>jnani</i> or knower of the Supreme Lord is  the  most  sublime  state  of
consciousness, more exalted than any other.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
This <i>avyakta</i> or unmanifest is beyond the perception of the mind  and  senses
and is <i>aksara</i> or  indestructible  and  contains  the  <i>brahman</i>  or  spiritual
substratum pervading all existence. In the second chapter  it  was  revealed
that the <i>atma</i> or soul is indestructible as well. But <i>aksara</i>    is  known  by
those knowledgeable of the Vedic scriptures to  be  paramount,  higher  than
the  <i>atma,</i>  the  goal  of  the  <i>atma</i>  as  verified  in  the  Katha  Upanisad
I.II.XXIII: That the realised eternal soul is the Supreme Lords  abode.  The
unrealised soul  tainted  by  association  with  <i>prakriti</i>  or  the  material
substratum pervading physical existence. But His presence as the witness  in
the hearts of all living beings is to keep them  always  connected  to  Him,
for the Brihadaranyaka Upanisad III:VII,XXII states: The Supreme  Being  has
established Himself in the heart of all living entities. The same  scripture
in III.III.VII states: That <i>paramatma</i> the supreme soul which resides in  the
heart of all living entities  is  different  from  the  individual  <i>atma</i>  or
soul; yet the <i>atma</i> knows Hin not, He whose abode is  the  <i>atma.</i>  It  is  the
Supreme Lord who is installed in the hearts of all beings as the witness  of
all thoughts and actions. Attaining Him the individual <i>atma</i>  is  permanently
divested of <i>prakriti</i> and they never return to the worlds of mortals  as  the
cycle of birth and death is terminated. The purport is that <i>atma  tattva</i>  or
realisation of the soul is very exalted but the  <i>paramam  gatim</i>  or  supreme
goal is to attain the Supreme  Lord  Krishna  in  His  <i>dhama</i>  or  abode  and
associate eternally with Him.
<p>
