<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The  question  may  arise  that  if  the  <i>brahman</i>  or  spiritual  substratum
pervading all existence is  neither  existing  or  non-existing  then  there
seems to be a contradiction with Vedic  scriptures  such  as  the  Chandogya
Upanisad III.XIV.I which state: Everything is nothing but  the  <i>brahman</i>  and
the Narasingha Upanisad VII.III which state: All this  is  nothing  but  the
<i>brahman.</i> Apprehending such doubts the nature and qualities  of  the  <i>brahman</i>
are revealed by Lord Krishna as being omnipresent, with heads, faces,  eyes,
ears, hands and feet everywhere. This manifests  through  the  inconceivable
potency and incomprehensible power of the Supreme Lord as confirmed  in  the
Svetavastara   Upanisad   VI.VIII   which   states:   The   Supreme    Lords
transcendental power is unlimited and manifold and His  knowledge,  strength
and action are inherent within Himself. The Supreme Lord who manifesting  in
all <i>jivas</i> or embodied beings has head, faces, eyes,  ears,  hands  and  feet
everywhere is pervading everything in creation  and  is  the  basis  of  all
activity as these selfsame organs of perception  and  locomotion  belong  to
all beings.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna begins this verse with  <i>sarvatah  pani-padam  tat</i>  meaning  the
Supreme Lords has hands and  feet  everywhere.  This  denotes  that  by  His
potency the Supreme Lord is capable of doing everywhere what all  hands  and
feet do in the embodied state. Similarly  He speaks <i>sarvatah  srutimal  loke</i>
meaning He hears everything in all  directions  internally  and  externally.
This denotes that He is capable of performing the functions  of  the  senses
such as hearing and seeing without the need of  any  physical  sense  organ.
The Supreme Lord known as <i>Parabrahman</i>  although  possessing  not  hands  and
feet is able to perform the activities of hands and feet.  The  Svetasvatara
Upanisad III.IXX beginning <i>apani pado jivana grihita</i> meaning:  Footless  and
handless He moves and grasps; eyeless and earless He sees  and  hears.  That
the <i>atma</i> or immortal soul of  every  sentient  being  even  situated  as  an
infinitesimal particle of <i>Parabrahman</i> also has the  capacity  of  performing
functions without organs as well. This is confirmed in the Mundaka  Upanisad
III.I.VIII beginning <i>tatha vidva punya-pape vidhuya niranjana  param  samyam</i>
<i>upaiti</i> means: The <i>atma</i> casting off the dross of merit  and  demerit  becomes
purified attaining qualitative sameness in transcendence as the <i>brahman.</i>  So
in conclusion the <i>ksetra-jna</i> abides  throughout  all  creation  encompassing
everything. This means  that  the  <i>atma</i>  pervades  all  things  in  creation
without exception for the <i>atma</i> has no limitations of  time  and  space.  The
<i>atma</i> is qualitatively equated  to   <i>Parabrahman</i>  but  quantitatively  it  is
minuscule similar to a ray of light from the sun.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
Lord Krishna defines the qualities of <i>Parabrahman</i> the Supreme  Lord  as  the
source of the  <i>brahman</i>  or  spiritual  substratum  pervading  all  existence
beginning with <i>sarvatah pani padam</i> meaning with hands and  feet  everywhere.
The Svetasvatara Upanisad III.IX states: Without feet or hands He  is  swift
and seizes. He sees  without  eyes  and  hears  without  the  ears.  Without
material organs the Supreme Lord sees on all  sides  and  hears  all  things
within and without and grasps from all directions. The  Supreme  Lord  being
of  transcendental  nature  endowed  with  supra-extraordinary  potency   is
performing all actions of coming, going, grasping, seizing, hearing,  seeing
although without hands and  eyes  and  ears  and  feet,  etc.  The  <i>jiva</i>  or
embodied being who realises <i>atma-tattva</i> or the reality  of  their  soul  and
achieves <i>moksa</i> or liberation from material existence, they  will  ultimately
attain the association of the Supreme Lord  and  experience  this  state  as
well. The Mundaka Upanisad III.I.III states: When an aspirant perceives  the
source of the <i>brahman</i> or spiritual substratum  pervading  all  existence  as
the brilliantly effulgent Supreme Lord, then realising  the  ultimate  truth
such a one becomes pristine and pure casting off all dualities such as  what
is good and what is evil and attains  direct  communion  with  the  Ultimate
Consciousness and the Supreme Lord. Hence all  those  liberated  beings  who
have attained such communion to the  Supreme  Lord  are  also  known  to  be
performing actions like Him irrespective of whether or  not  they  have  the
actual material organs. Through the auspices of the  <i>atma</i>  or  immotal  soul
and the <i>brahman</i> the <i>Parabrahman</i> pervades with knowledge all objects  of  the
<i>ksetra-jna.</i> When ignorance has been completely  removed  and  nescience  has
been transcended the unlimited aspect of the <i>atma</i> becomes manifest  and  the
quality of knowledge becomes unlimited and the soul becomes endowed with  an
all pervasive quality of omniscience including past and  future.  Only  then
is the <i>atma</i> spoken of as being equated to <i>Parabrahman;</i> otherwise it  is  not
possible. The same is declared by Vedavyasa in his treatise  Vedanta  Sutras
II.III.XXVIII beginning <i>yavadatam  bhavitva ca</i> meaning: Proof that the  <i>atma</i>
is the consciousness factor is that cognition is only  present  as  long  as
the <i>atma</i> is present; before  self  realisation  it  is  obscured  and  after
enlightenment it is perceived.
