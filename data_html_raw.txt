<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The question may arise that do the other three classes being  the  <i>artta</i>  or
afflicted, the <i>artharthi</i> or those desiring wealth and the <i>jijnasuh</i> or  those
desiring self realisation have to undergo transmigration and  reincarnation?
The answer is never. This is  confirmed  by  Lord  Krishna  using  the  word
<i>udarah</i> meaning noble and magnanimous. All these noble  beings  obtain  <i>moksa</i>
or liberation from the cycle of birth and death in the  material  existence;
but the <i>jnani</i> or knower of God is His very <i>atma</i> or  soul.  This  is  because
the <i>jnani</i> with his mind fixed  solely  upon  the  Supreme  Lord,  has  taken
complete refuge in Him alone as the only goal  other  than  which  there  is
nothing superior.  So such a  realised  being  is  interested  in  no  other
purpose then attaining the Supreme Lord and thus devotes all  there  efforts
for this.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Due to the fact that the previous four classes all  resort  to  the  Supreme
Lord, they are all noble and <i>udarah</i> or magnanimous  inasmuch  as  when  they
receive any benediction from Him it is as by their loving act of  acceptance
that the Supreme Lord  feels  bliss  and  is  truly  grateful  to  them  for
accepting His love. Lord Krishna considers the <i>jnani</i> or the  knower  of  God
as dear to Him as His own self and considers even that  His  very  existence
depends upon them. One may question how is this to be understood?  One  must
understand that the Supreme Lord reciprocates in  the  same  manner  as  His
devotee acts towards Him. Thus in the same way that His devotee cannot  live
without Him, He cannot live without His devotee. Therefore His devotee  <i>atma</i>
or soul realised is His very life as the <i>atma.</i>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
One may pose the question what about the other three types being  <i>artto</i>  the
aflicted, <i>atharthi</i> or those seeking wealth and <i>jnajinsuh</i>  or  those  seeking
<i>moksa</i> or liberation. Are they wretched and unworthy?  To  the  contrary  and
Lord  Krishna  confirms  this  with  the  word  <i>udarah</i>  meaning  noble   and
magnanimous. All these devotees are  noble  beings  who  have    accumulated
great merit in the course of tens of thousands of previous  births.  No  one
can become a devotee of Lord Krishna if they only have  a  small  amount  of
merit from previous lives. The devotees of the  Supreme  Lord  Krishna  have
such a surplus of accumulated merit due  to  having  practised  austerities,
undergone penance, performed yoga, engaged in  meditation,  offered  prayers
and given worship. That it is a natural  course  of  events  that  in  their
early lives they attain the opportunity to be Lord Krishna's devotee.   This
is confirmed and corroborated in the  Vedic  scriptures  by  the  statement:
Humans whose sins are expired cultivate devotion  to  Lord  Krishna.  Others
who still remain sinful never become Lord Krishna's devotee, they  only  can
worship Kali, Ganesh or some impersonal phantasm conception.  So  for  those
aspirants of the three  previous  types  who  have  the  tinge  of  fruitive
motivations, still they are the elect and after finally  achieving  devotion
to Him they become free from desire and qualified for  <i>moksa</i>  or  liberation
from the cycle of birth and death. Lord Krishna accepts  the  <i>jnani</i>  as  His
very self to exemplify their  exalted  position,  because  nothing  is  more
dearer then oneself. For the <i>jnani</i> is the elite  and  the  absolute  devotee
who is firmly established being time tested over a myriad of  lifetimes.  He
is wholeheartedly devoted to the Supreme Lord as his only  goal  another  of
which there is nothing superior and having such faith assuredly  arrives  at
that goal.
<p>
