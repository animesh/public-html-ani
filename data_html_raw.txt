<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
What qualifies a person to be qualified  for  <i>sannyasa</i>  or  renunciation  in
abnegation for whom cessation of  activities  is  prescribed?  Lord  Krishna
explains that when one is no  longer  enamoured  by  the  pleasures  of  the
senses and the sense objects and is also not even  attracted  to  performing
the actions which are the means to obtain them, such a person  is  qualified
for <i>dhyana yoga</i> or the science of  the  individual  consciousness  attaining
communion with the ultimate consciousness by mediation. This is because  one
must develop the renunciation habitually  until  the  dross  of  desires  is
eradicated along with not the slightest inclination to perform any  activity
that will lead to enjoyment of sense objects and the opportunity  for  sense
gratification completely neutralising all desires. Then a  person  is  known
to be have factually attained <i>dhyana yoga.</i>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
In this verse Lord Krishna tells  the  signs  of  one  who  has  established
themselves in equanimity of mind.  For  such  a  person  there  is  absolute
detachment because there is no desire for results. If one is devoted to  the
Supreme Lord then all imperfections dissolved on their own. If  one  is  not
devoted then they are eradicated by special effort.
<p>
Now begins the summation.
<p>
How does one become detached from one's actions? The answer is  to  renounce
the desire for the rewards of actions or if one is devoted  to  the  Supreme
Lord to offer all the rewards to Him. By acting in either of these ways  one
is considered performing renunciation for the Supreme Lord.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna uses the compound word <i>yogarudhas</i> which is an adept  in  <i>dhyana</i>
<i>yoga</i> or the science of  the  individual  consciousness  attaining  communion
with  the  ultimate  consciousness  by  meditation.  Such  a   person   from
experiencing the sublime bliss of the <i>atma</i> or soul within has ceased  to  be
infatuated by sensual objects and is no longer deluded by  the  impulses  of
the senses in relation to  such  sense  objects.  The  words  <i>na  anusajjate</i>
meaning not enamoured denotes that one is no longer under the  influence  of
such delusions. One is <i>yogarudhas</i> who has abandoned all illusions and  false
conceptions. Thus for the aspirant of  <i>moksa</i>  or  liberation  from  material
existence who still is under the sway of infatuation and delusion  there  is
no alternative but to perform <i>karma yoga</i> or prescribed Vedic  activities  as
the means to relinquish oneself from from dross of  bodily  association  and
carnal desires. This being accomplished one  has  qualified  themselves  for
<i>dhyana yoga</i> and can begin to perfect their  meditation.  Hence  as  warning,
before this point it is recommended to exclusively perform <i>karma yoga</i>  until
completely free from the infatuation of desires and sense objects.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
If one wants to understand exactly when performing <i>karma yoga</i> or  prescribed
Vedic activities transforms itself to the heights  of  <i>dhyana  yoga</i>  or  the
science of the individual soul attaining communion with  the  ultimate  soul
by meditation is now revealed. Lord Krishna speaks  the  words:  <i>na  karmasi</i>
<i>anusajjate</i> meaning when one no longer craves or  is  inclined  to  make  any
effort for sense objects. Such a  person  thinks  why  should  I  strive  to
obtain  pleasures  which  are  here  today  and  gone  tomorrow  being  only
temporary?  Then  with  constant  endeavour   one   ceases   to   look   for
opportunities  to  facilitate  favourable  circumstances  to  enjoy  sensual
pleasures and evenually evaporates all thoughts of  enjoying  sense  objects
along with dissolving the memories  of  previous  enjoyments.  Only  such  a
being is considered to be firmly established in <i>yoga</i> or the science  of  the
individual soul attaining communion with the ultimate consciousness.
<p>
