<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify>Now  Lord  Krishna  glorifies  the  auspicious  sound  vibration  TAT.   The
performance of  <i>yagna</i> or propitiation, <i>tapah</i> or  austerities  and  <i>danam</i>  or
charity by the pure minded aspirants in <i>sattva guna</i>  the  mode  of  goodness
are performed without any desire for rewards. Therefore  the  recitation  of
TAT is auspicious because through the  purification  of  the  mind  and  the
subsequent renunciation of desire for recompense one becomes  motivated  for
spiritual goals such as <i>moksa</i> or liberation from  material  existence,  <i>atma</i>
<i>tattva</i> or realisation of the immortal soul and communion  with  the  Supreme
Lord.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Whatever  prescribed  Vedic  activities  performed  by  the  Vaisnavas   and
brahmanas such as study of the Vedic scriptures and  <i>yagna</i>  or  propitiation
and worship by ritual to the Supreme  Lord,  <i>tapah</i>  or  austerities  by  the
<i>ksatriyas</i> or royal warrior class and <i>danam</i> or  charity  by  the  <i>vaisyas</i>  or
merchant class are all done out of devotion and  dedicated  to  the  Supreme
Lord Krishna or  any  of  His  authorised  incarnations  and  expansions  as
revealed in Vedic scriptures  without  the  slightest  desire  for  material
rewards. Such activities  devoid  of  motivation  for  mundane  results  are
designated as  TAT  signifying  that  such  acts  are  for  <i>atma  tattva</i>  or
realisation of the immortal soul, perception of  the  <i>brahman</i>  or  spiritual
substratum pervading all existence and attaining communion with the  Supreme
Lord. The Vishnu Sahasrasa confirms that:  <i>sah,  vah,  kah,  klim,</i>  and  <i>tat</i>
denote  the  Supreme  absolute.  Hence  the  ontology  of   TAT   has   been
demonstrated  to  signify  its  connection  to  transcendental  and  eternal
activities  which  independently  bestow  <i>moksa</i>  or  freedom  from  material
existence and promotion to the eternal spiritual realms.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
The auspiciousness of resonating TAT also  as  a  name  of  the  <i>brahman</i>  or
spiritual substratum pervading all existence which  is  a  potency  of  Lord
Krishna signifying that everything is under  the  complete  control  of  the
Supreme Lord. To perform prescribed Vedic activities without any desire  for
rewards is known as TAT. Such followers of the Vedic scriptures perform  all
activities for the Supreme Lord out of devotion or as a matter of duty  with
no expectation of anything in return. This is also known as TAT.
<p>
