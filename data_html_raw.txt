<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The proposed means together with its accessories is being stated by in  this
verse and the  next.  Lord  Krishna  uses  the  word  <i>samyamya</i>  which  means
controlling. Withdrawing all the inlets of  the  organs  being  eyes,  ears,
tongue, etc. by non-cognition of external objects and focusing the  mind  in
the heart, dismissing all external conceptions while fixing the  life  force
in the <i>ajna cakra</i> between the eyebrows one should establish  themselves  and
become absorbed in <i>yoga</i> or  the  science  of  the  individual  consciousness
attaining communion with the ultimate consciousness.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
Lord Krishna uses the words <i>sarva-dvarani  samyamya</i>  means  controlling  the
passages which enter into the body.  This  denotes  withdrawing  the  senses
from  external  cognition  or  restricting  the  senses  from  their  normal
outgoing tendencies. The words <i>hrdi-nirudhya</i> means locking the mind  in  the
heart. It denotes directing devotional feelings from the mind  to  the  <i>atma</i>
or soul in the heart and  offering  them  to  the  <i>aksaram</i>  or  imperishable
Supreme Lord enthroned there. The  words  <i>yoga-dharanam</i>  or  performing  the
science  of  the  individual  consciousness  attaining  communion  with  the
ultimate consciousness in  unwavering  meditation.  This  denotes  concerted
concentration in perfectly meditating upon the Supreme  Lord  with  complete
attention.
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna describes the method  in  brief  with  details  with  the  word
<i>samyamya</i>  meaning  restrained.  Having  restrained   all   the   senses   by
withdrawing them from their respective objects, all the doors  of  the  body
such as the eyes, ears, etc.,  along  with  the  cessation  of  all  worldly
considerations, fixing the mind in the heart and directing the  life  breath
to the <i>ajna cakra</i> between the eyebrows one so becomes  established  in  <i>yoga</i>
or the science of the individual consciousness attaining communion with  the
ultimate consciousness.
<p>
