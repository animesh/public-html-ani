<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
Lord Krishna concludes this  exposition  by  stating  that  when  prescribed
Vedic activities and obligatory rites which are  compulsory  for  all  human
beings such as following <i>ekadasi</i> which is fasting from all grains and  beans
on the 11th day during both the waxing and waning moon and is  performed  in
knowledge as a matter of duty without any attachment or desire  for  rewards
is known to be clearly situated in <i>sattva guna</i> the  mode  of  goodness.  The
Skanda  Purana  beginning  <i>matraha  pitraha  caiva  bhatraha  guruha   tatha</i>
<i>ekadasyantu</i> confirms that all who eat grains on <i>ekadasi</i>  can  never  achieve
liberation from material existence and attain  communion  with  the  Supreme
Lord. So it is for the ultimate benefit of  all  human  beings  that  it  is
compulsory not to eat grains on <i>ekadasi</i> and this axiom applies also  to  all
the Vedic ordinances and injunctions.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><font size=2>There is no commentary for this verse.</font>
</td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
It should be understood and clearly comprehended that all  prescribed  Vedic
activities  both  <i>nitya</i>  or  eternal  and  <i>naimittika</i>  or   occasional   are
obligatory duties for aspirants seeking <i>moksa</i> or  liberation  from  material
existence and communion with the Supreme Lord. These  prescribed  activities
devolve into the specific duties <i>varna</i> and <i>asrama</i> or  the  stage  and  class
one is situated in. This may be initiated by birth  but  it  is  established
inherently and qualified by deeds. Actual renunciation  is  performing  such
prescribed Vedic activities free from  egoism,  without  any  attachment  or
desire for any rewards. This is real renunciation and is situated in  <i>sattva</i>
<i>guna</i> the  mode  of  goodness  where  the  source  of  spiritual  realisation
commences; properly situated in correct  knowledge,  untainted  by  delusion
and free from illusion. This theme will appear again in verse 30.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Now Lord  Krishna  reveals  renunciation  characterised  with  the  mode  of
goodness. Prescribed Vedic activities performed as <i>karyam iti  eva</i>  a  sense
of duty and <i>tvaktya sangam</i> abandoning attachment to  receiving  rewards  for
one's actions and any and all ego sense are situated in <i>sattva guna</i>  because
they are performed for the satisfaction of the Supreme Lord by the grace  of
the Vaisnava preceptor<i>.</i> This includes both daily activities  and  occasional
activities. The purport is that  activities  performed  with  an  intent  of
offering them to the Supreme Lord  purifies the  consciousness  and  bestows
spiritual knowledge.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 18, verse 9 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
