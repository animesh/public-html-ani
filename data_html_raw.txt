<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The <i>sraddha</i> or faith in the Supreme Lord of one who acts in accordance  with
the ordinances and injunctions of the Vedic scriptures  is  always  situated
in <i>sattva guna</i> the mode of goodness. The <i>sraddha</i> existing in the  other  two
modes of <i>raja guna</i> the mode of passion and <i>tama guna</i> the mode  of  ignorance
are the results of one's  nature  by  the  impressions  lingering  in  their
consciousness from  past  life  activities  in  such  modes.  Only  absolute
knowledge  from  the  Vedic  scriptures  duly  instructed  by  the  Vaisnava
spiritual master has the potency to affect change in the nature  of  a  <i>jiva</i>
or embodied being. But those in <i>raja guna</i> and <i>tama guna</i> who  have  no  faith
in the Vedic scriptures or who have no  interest  in  the  Vedic  scriptures
will not be able to change  their  natures  to  <i>sattva  guna</i>  by  any  other
process.  Hence  their  faith  arises  from  whatever  characteristics  they
possessed in the previous life and this manifests in their actions.
<p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The <i>gunas</i> or three modes of material nature are  <i>sattva  guna</i>  the  mode  of
goodnes<i>, raja guna</i> the mode of passion and <i>tama guna</i> the mode of  ignorance.
They are singularly inherent within  the  faith  of  all  mankind  and  they
conform to the characteristics and disposition particular to  each  <i>jiva</i>  or
embodied being.  The  impressions  from  past  life  incarnations  arise  as
tendencies pursued in a present life correspondent to one's mood  and  taste
and from that mood and taste ones' faith arises. Displays of enthusiasm  are
indications of faith where underlying is the belief that the effort will  be
successfully fulfilled. Tendency, taste and faith  are  actually  properties
of the <i>atma</i> or immortal soul;  but  they  are  only  evoked  when  the  <i>atma</i>
happens by chance to come in contact with the <i>gunas.</i>  It  has  been  deduced
that when there is tranquillity in the mind there is control of  the  senses
and peacefulness of the body and such effect manifests  the  chance  contact
between the <i>atma</i> and the <i>gunas</i> resulting in <i>sattva guna.</i>  Agitation  of  the
mind, unbridled senses and over indulgence of the body results in  <i>raja</i>  and
<i>tama guna.</i> Thus  effectively the experiences from past lives  manifest  into
one of the three <i>gunas.</i> 
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Lord Krishna now elaborates on how <i>sraddha</i> differentiates itself  under  the
auspices of one of the three <i>gunas</i> or modes of  material  nature  which  are
<i>sattva guna</i> the mode of goodness<i>, raja guna</i> the mode  of  passion  and  <i>tama</i>
<i>guna</i> the mode of ignorance. These three archetypes apply to all  beings  and
all things within the material nature. Those who have no  knowledge  of  the
Vedic scriptures and are indifferent to them languish in  <i>tama  guna.</i>  Those
who have some knowledge of the Vedic scriptures yet chose to disrespect  and
oppose them due to insidious motives and nefarious intrigues are  bewildered
in <i>raja guna.</i> Those who faithfully follow the ordinances and injunctions  of
the Vedic scriptures are safely  situated  in  <i>sattva  guna</i>  due  to  having
performed  pious  activities  in  innumerable  past  lives  without  selfish
motivations. There faith is directed to the worship of the Supreme Lord  and
as such is conducive for <i>moksa</i> or liberation from material existence.
<p>
The inherent characteristics one possesses in present  life  are  determined
by activities performed in previous lives that  were  voluntarily  followed.
These innate characteristics catapult a <i>jiva</i> or embodied being into  one  of
the three <i>gunas</i> and the corresponding type of  faith  one  possesses  is  in
accordance to the <i>guna</i> they are situated in. Such faith regardless of  which
<i>guna</i> gives the confidence that by  it  one  will  accomplish  their  desired
goal.  When  there  is  confidence   in   accomplishing   successfully   any
undertaking, a <i>jiva</i> will engage wholeheartedly in it.  This  is  a  sign  of
faith. But there are  gradations  and  differences  of  quality  in  various
undertakings and these distinctions are what determines whether an  activity
is  in  <i>sattva,  raja</i>  or  <i>tama  guna.</i>  Those  who  have  no  knowledge   of
reincarnation, no knowledge of <i>karma</i> or reactions to actions,  no  knowledge
of the Vedic scriptures, nor knowledge of  the  paramount  position  of  the
Supreme  Lord  Krishna;  are  helplessly  indulging   themselves   only   in
transitory, mundane activities. Such activities no matter how  great,  noble
and well intentioned award no opportunity to free oneself from  <i>samsara</i>  the
perpetual cycle of birth and death and achieve <i>moksa.</i>
<p>
