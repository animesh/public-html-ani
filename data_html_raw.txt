<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
When the spiritual traditions and spiritual values are destroyed in
society, unrighteousness predominates and the surviving family members
become degraded.
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<i><u>Madhvacarya has no commentary so we present Baladeva Vidyabhusana's.</u></i>
<p>
Now the evil consequences of war are described in  detail  with  this  verse
beginning <i>kula-ksaye pranasyanti.</i> The  compound  word  <i>kula-dharmah</i>  meaning
the righteous family traditions prescribed in Vedic rites such as  the  fire
ceremony <i>agnihotra</i> and others of this nature due to the destruction  of  the
dynasty causes a lack of qualified family members  knowledgeable  enough  to
have them performed. When <i>dharma</i> or righteousness  is  absent  then  <i>adharma</i>
unrighteousness appears apprehending the  remaining  dependants  along  with
the whole family destroying the foundations of society.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><font size=2>There is no commentary for this verse.</font>
</td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Some may ponder just what sin is Arjuna describing and this is now  revealed
in this verse. In a dynasty it is the father that is the foundation  of  the
family. He insures that the tradition of the members is maintained  as  well
being the support of all the members. In times of war it is the  father  who
goes to fight and sometimes the older  sons  who  are  the  fathers  of  the
future also go to war. If they are slain in battle  it  is  understood  that
the family is effectively destroyed and righteousness along  with  the  age-
old family customs and Vedic  traditions  eventually  cease  to  exist.  The
women and children not being properly protected, having   lost  the  shelter
of the father are thus overcome by  the  realities  of  basic  survival  and
become victims of unrighteousness.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 1, verse 39 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
