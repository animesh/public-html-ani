<br><div align=center><h2>Commentaries of the Four Authorized Vaisnava Sampradayas</h2><b>as confirmed in the Garga Samhita Canto 10, Chapter 61, Verses 23, 24, 25, 26</b></div><table width=100% cellspacing=16 cellpadding=6><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Rudra Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic06-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Visnuswami</font></td></tr></table><center>Sridhara Swami's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The unparalleled and incomparable  nature  of  the  Supreme  Lord  Krishna's
<i>visvarupa</i> or divine universal form is now being stated.  The  effulgence  of
this universal form is being compared to the simultaneous rising  of  <i>surya-</i>
<i>sahasrasya</i> or 1000 suns. This is to give a general idea of  the  magnificent
splendour Lord Krishna's phenomenal universal form manifested. This  affirms
that there is nothing else to compare with the  <i>visvarupa</i>  in  the  material
existence and gives the evidence from what was exhibited in verse nine.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Brahma Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic05-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Madhvacarya</font></td></tr></table><center>Madhvacarya's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
The word  <i>sahasraya</i>  means  thousand  but  can  literally  be  taken  to  be
innumerable  as  1000  suns  simultaneously  rising  in  the  sky  would  be
uncountable. Like the thousands of heroic  deeds  of  the  <i>avatars</i>  of  Lord
Krishna,  this  statement  is  made  for  elucidation  of  the   resplendent
splendour of the <i>visvarupa</i> or divine universal form.  In  the  Rig  Veda  is
stated: The Supreme Lord of eternal power and eternal energy is  of  eternal
brilliance even easily exceeding the sun. This comparison is  based  on  the
conclusive evidence of the Vedic scriptures and it is of no avail to try  to
compare it to anything else.
<p>
</div></td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Sri Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic07-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Ramanuja</font></td></tr></table><center>Ramanuja's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
<p>
The example of a 1000 suns is a mere illustration  to  show  the  degree  of
infinite splendour and radiance that the form of  Lord  Krishna's  <i>visvarupa</i>
or divine universal form displayed in ever increasing measure.
<p>
<p>
</div></td><td style="border: 1px solid" bgcolor=#404080 valign=top width=50%><div align=center><u>Kumara Vaisnava Sampradaya:</u></div><p><table align=left hspace=20 vspace=8 ><tr><td align=center><img hspace=8 src="/DiscipleSuccession/pics/icons/pic08-2.jpg" width=60><br><font size=1>Nimbaditya</font></td></tr></table><center>Kesava Kasmiri's Commentary</center><p><div align=justify><p>
Now the effulgence of Lord Krishna's <i>visvarupa</i> or divine universal  form  is
being described. The word <i>divi</i> means in the firmament. If in the  heavens  a
thousand suns burst forth simultaneously  all  at  once  it  could  give  an
indication of the dazzling radiance and splendour the universal form and  no
other comparison is suitable. As 1000 suns rising simultaneously  cannot  be
envisioned except by imagination and is practically  an  impossible  reality
it is difficult to envision. Thus the comparison infers that  the  universal
form is beyond compare to anything ever seen or imagined.
<p>
</div></td></tr></table><center>Thus ends commentaries of chapter 11, verse 12 of the Srimad Bhagavad-Gita.</center>
